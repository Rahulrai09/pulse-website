How to Report a Medical Device Adverse Event in India

A pump gives the wrong dose, a lead breaks, a stent kit arrives with a torn pack. This guide shows hospitals what to report under India's device safety programme, how fast, and how to set up the process.

What Is MvPI?

MvPI is India's national programme for medical device safety. It started in 2015 and is run by the Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC), which collects reports through a network of more than 775 monitoring centres. An "adverse event" is any harm, or risk of harm, linked to a medical device, even if no one is sure the device caused it.

Reporting is rising fast. Data released on 6 November 2026 showed serious device-related events grew from 808 in 2020 to 2,176 in 2025, a total of 9,373 since 2020.

What to Report

  • Deaths or serious harm where a device may have played a part.
  • Device faults and near misses, even when no patient was hurt.
  • Wrong readings or test results, software errors and mistakes in use.
  • Damaged packs, wrong labels and design flaws.
  • Any case where staff are not sure. The guidance says to report it anyway.

Reporting Timelines

Type of eventReport within
Serious event: death or serious harm to health15 days

These timelines come from the IPC's Version 1.3 hospital guidance, released with WHO India in September 2026.

How to Report

  • ADRMS portal: the IPC's online system. Doctors, nurses and biomedical staff report here.
  • Form by email: fill in the Medical Device Adverse Event Reporting Form and email it to mvpi.ipcindia@gmail.com, or send it to your nearest monitoring centre.
  • Helpline: call 1800 180 3024 (toll-free).
  • Patients and families can tell their doctor, call the helpline, or use the consumer version of the form.

Sending a report does not mean the hospital, the maker or the device was at fault. The form also says patient names are kept private.

What the Form Asks For

SectionWhat to fill in
ReporterWho is reporting and whether the maker has been told
A. DeviceBrand name, maker, model, lot or batch number, serial number, software version, last service and calibration dates
B. EventDate, place, who was using the device, what happened and what was done with the device
C. PatientAge, sex, weight, history and outcome
D. HospitalName and a contact person
E and FCause found and the maker's action, for follow-up reports

Most of this comes from your device records. If each device already has a log with its batch, serial number and calibration dates, a report takes minutes, not days. See our NABH medical equipment requirements guide for the records to keep.

Set Up Reporting in Your Hospital

  1. Name a nodal officer for the hospital, and a contact person in each department.
  2. Make reporting easy: a simple form, an email address or a group chat where staff can flag a problem.
  3. Check and upload: the nodal officer checks each report and uploads it to the ADRMS portal on time.
  4. Train staff often, especially nurses, resident doctors and biomedical engineers.
  5. List critical devices in the ICU, OT, cath lab and emergency, and review them weekly or monthly by risk.
  6. Keep the device and its packing for checks, and keep service logs and the cause analysis on file.
  7. Tell the supplier so the maker can investigate and issue a safety notice if needed.

Medical Device Adverse Event Reporting: FAQs

How do I report a medical device adverse event in India?

Report it to the Materiovigilance Programme of India (MvPI). Use the ADRMS portal, email the reporting form to mvpi.ipcindia@gmail.com, send it to your nearest monitoring centre, or call the helpline 1800 180 3024.

What is the time limit for reporting a serious device adverse event?

Under the IPC hospital guidance of September 2026, serious events such as a death or serious harm must be reported within 15 days. Other events that could cause harm must be reported within 30 days.

Should we report a device fault if no patient was harmed?

Yes. Near misses, faults, wrong readings, damaged packs and wrong labels should all be recorded and reported, even when no one was hurt.

What is MvPI?

MvPI is the Materiovigilance Programme of India. It started in 2015, is run by the Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission and collects reports of harm linked to medical devices through more than 775 monitoring centres.

Does reporting an adverse event mean the hospital was at fault?

No. The reporting form states that a report does not mean the hospital, the maker or the device caused the event, and patient names are kept private.
