Flying With an Electric Wheelchair in India: Battery Rules and Tips

Electric wheelchairs can fly, but their lithium batteries follow airline safety rules. This guide explains the 300 Wh limit, how to read your battery, what Indian airlines ask for, and how to get the chair ready at check-in.

The Basic Rules

Battery rules for wheelchairs come from the international dangerous goods rules that airlines follow. IATA's 2026 guidance and Air India's own page set out the main points:

SituationRule
Airline approvalNeeded for every battery-powered wheelchair; ask in advance
Lithium battery left in the chair, well protectedChair goes in the hold; no Wh limit
Lithium battery removedMust not exceed 300 Wh; carried in the cabin, protected from damage
Spare batteriesCarried in the cabin; IATA allows spares up to 300 Wh in total per chair
Spillable (wet) batteriesNot accepted by Air India or IndiGo

Some airlines still use the older spare rule of one battery up to 300 Wh or two up to 160 Wh each, so check with yours.

How to Work Out Your Battery's Wh

Look for the Wh rating printed on the battery. If it is not there, multiply volts by amp-hours:

Wh = volts × Ah. A 24 V, 10 Ah battery is 240 Wh. A 24 V, 20 Ah battery is 480 Wh, which is above the limit for a removed battery.

What Indian Airlines Ask For

  • Air India: fill in its Battery Powered Wheelchair Information Form at least 48 hours before departure, carry a copy, and arrive about three hours early.
  • IndiGo: electric wheelchairs are taken apart at the check-in counter. Carry the wheelchair or battery manual. IndiGo asks for wheelchair assistance requests 48 hours ahead.

Rules change, so we suggest calling the airline after booking and again a day before you fly.

Getting the Chair Ready

  • Turn the power off: remove the key or joystick, or unplug the battery connector.
  • Freewheel mode: switch it on so staff can push the chair.
  • Cover the terminals: use caps or insulating tape and a protective pouch for any removed battery.
  • Take small parts with you: cushions, the joystick and headrests travel in the cabin.
  • Carry details: make, model, battery type, Wh rating, weight and size, and how to remove the battery.

These steps follow IATA's 2026 guidance. For day-to-day battery care, see our electric wheelchair battery care guide.

Flying With an Electric Wheelchair: Key Facts

  • Airlines must approve every battery-powered wheelchair before travel.
  • A removed lithium wheelchair battery must not exceed 300 Wh and goes in the cabin.
  • An installed, protected lithium battery has no Wh limit.
  • Wh equals volts times amp-hours; 24 V 10 Ah is 240 Wh.
  • Air India asks for its battery form at least 48 hours before departure.

Flying With an Electric Wheelchair: FAQs

Can I take an electric wheelchair on a flight in India?

Yes, with the airline's approval. Lithium and non-spillable batteries are accepted under safety rules; spillable batteries are not.

What is the battery limit for an electric wheelchair on a plane?

A removed lithium battery must not exceed 300 Wh. If the battery stays installed and protected, there is no Wh limit.

How do I find my wheelchair battery's Wh?

Check the label, or multiply volts by amp-hours. A 24 V 12 Ah battery is 288 Wh.

How early should I tell the airline?

Air India asks for its Battery Powered Wheelchair Information Form at least 48 hours before departure. We suggest contacting any airline as early as possible.

Where does the removed battery go?

In the cabin with you, protected from damage, with its terminals covered to prevent short circuits.
