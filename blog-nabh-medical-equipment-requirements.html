NABH Medical Equipment Requirements: A Checklist for Hospitals

Getting ready for NABH, or buying for a hospital that will apply? This guide shows what the new rules ask for medical devices, the records to keep, and what to get from your supplier on day one.

What NABH Asks For

NABH is the national body that accredits hospitals in India. It released the 6th edition of its hospital rules in September 2024, and they have applied since 1 January 2025. Medical devices sit in the chapter on facility safety (FMS), under FMS.5. Here is what each part asks for.

FMS.5 elementWhat it meansProof to keep
5a. PlanningChoose equipment to match the services you offerEquipment plan; purchase committee minutes
5b. Inventory and logsKnow every device you own and its historyInventory list; a log or history sheet per device
5c. Operation and maintenance planPlanned preventive and breakdown maintenancePM schedule, PM checklists, breakdown plan
5d. Inspection and calibrationCheck that devices measure and work correctlyCalibration certificates, schedule and labels
5e. Qualified and trained staffUsers and service staff know the equipmentTraining records; biomedical staff qualifications
5f. Replacement and disposalWritten rules for when and how to retire devicesCondemnation policy and disposal records
5g. Adverse events and recallsAct on recall notices and report device problemsRecall file; adverse event reports
5h. Downtime of critical equipmentTrack how long critical devices are out of useComplaint register with start and end times

A hospital training summary marks 5c as a core part and 5h as a higher-level part. Each hospital must name its own list of key devices. Examples include ventilators, anaesthesia machines, monitors, the cath lab, CT, MRI, ultrasound and lab machines.

Records Checklist

A biomedical team checklist for NABH lists these records. Keep them for every device:

  • Purchase papers: the bill, the licence, the quality mark and the maker's test report.
  • Log: the date you bought it, each service and check, each upgrade and each spare part fitted.
  • Planned service: a schedule that you follow, and a checklist used at each visit.
  • Repair plan: who checks a broken device and who fixes it, under warranty, AMC or CMC.
  • Calibration: a report, a schedule, and a label on the device with the last and next dates.
  • Training: a record of who was trained on each device and how to use it safely.
  • Complaints: a register with the time each fault was reported, seen and fixed.
  • Contracts: a list of which devices are under warranty, AMC or CMC.

The same checklist asks for a set time to respond to faults, with ICU and OT first, and help at any hour for key devices.

Adverse Events and Recalls

India tracks harm from medical devices through a national programme called MvPI. It started on 6 July 2015. Since January 2018, the Medical Device Rules 2017 have required hospitals and makers to report such harm.

New MvPI advice, reported in September 2026, asks hospitals to report serious harm, such as a death, within 15 days. Other faults should be reported within 30 days, even before the cause is clear. Each hospital should name one person to lead this. Under NABH 5g, a recalled device must not be used on patients until the fault is fixed. What to Ask Your Supplier

Most of these records start on the day a device arrives. Ask every supplier for:

  • The CDSCO licence, quality marks and the maker's test report.
  • A calibration report when the device is set up, and a schedule for the next ones.
  • The maker's service schedule and checklist.
  • Training for your staff on day one, with a signed list of who came.
  • User and service manuals.
  • A written promise to tell you about recalls and safety alerts.
  • How fast they will respond, how much uptime they promise, and spare parts after the warranty.

NABH Medical Equipment Requirements: FAQs

What are the NABH requirements for medical equipment?

Under the 6th edition, FMS.5 asks for a plan, a list of every device with its log, a service plan, checks and calibration, trained staff, rules to retire old devices, action on recalls and faults, and a record of downtime for key devices.

Which NABH chapter covers medical equipment?

The chapter on facility safety, called FMS. In the 6th edition, FMS.5 covers medical devices.

What records does NABH need for each device?

Keep the purchase papers, a log, the service schedule and checklists, calibration reports and a calibration label, training records, a fault register and the warranty, AMC or CMC status.

When did the NABH 6th edition start?

NABH released the 6th edition hospital rules on 17 September 2024. They have applied since 1 January 2025.

Do hospitals have to report medical device adverse events?

Yes. Since 2018, the Medical Device Rules 2017 have required harm from devices to be reported to the national MvPI programme. New 2026 advice asks for serious harm to be reported within 15 days.
