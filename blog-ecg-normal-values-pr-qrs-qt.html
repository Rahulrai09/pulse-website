ECG Normal Values: PR Interval, QRS Interval and QT Explained

Every ECG report lists a few key timings: the PR interval, the QRS duration (also called the QRS interval) and the QT (or QTc). This guide explains what each one measures, the normal adult values, how to read them from the paper, and what long or short values can mean.

Reading the ECG Paper

At the standard paper speed, each small box is 0.04 seconds (40 ms) and each big box, made of 5 small boxes, is 0.2 seconds (200 ms). Intervals are measured from left to right. ECG machines with automatic measurement also print these values on the report.

PR Interval: Long or Short

  • Over 200 ms: first-degree AV block, a delay in the signal passing through the heart's AV node.
  • Under 120 ms: can mean an extra electrical pathway (such as Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome) or a rhythm starting near the AV node.

QRS: When Is It Wide?

  • 100 to 119 ms: a mild delay in conduction inside the ventricles.
  • 120 ms or more: suggests a bundle branch block.

QT and QTc

The QT runs from the start of the QRS to the end of the T wave. It gets shorter as the heart beats faster, so doctors use the corrected value, QTc. The most common formula, Bazett's, divides the QT by the square root of the time between beats (RR, in seconds). It over-corrects at fast heart rates and under-corrects at slow ones, so other formulas such as Fridericia are used outside 60 to 100 beats per minute.

  • QTc over 500 ms: linked to a higher risk of a dangerous rhythm called torsades de pointes.
  • QTc under 350 ms: abnormally short.
  • Common causes of a long QTc: low potassium, magnesium or calcium, some medicines, heart muscle ischaemia, very low body temperature and inherited long QT syndrome.

A quick rule of thumb: a normal QT is usually less than half the time between two heartbeats.

What a Normal ECG Report Says

Most ECG machines print a short summary at the top of the report. A normal adult report usually shows these points:

Item on the reportNormal adult finding
RhythmNormal sinus rhythm (NSR): regular, with a P wave before every QRS
Heart rate60 to 100 beats a minute
PR interval120 to 200 ms, and the same from beat to beat
QRSNarrow: under 120 ms, usually under 100 ms
AxisNormal, about −30° to +90°
ST segment and T wavesNo ST elevation or depression; T waves upright in most leads

"WNL" on a report means "within normal limits". A rate under 60 is called sinus bradycardia and over 100 is sinus tachycardia; both can be normal in some people, such as athletes or someone with a fever. The machine's printed comment is only a guide. We suggest always having a doctor read the full tracing, because a normal ECG at rest does not rule out every heart problem.

ECG Normal Values: Key Facts

  • A normal adult PR interval is 120 to 200 ms, or 3 to 5 small boxes.
  • A QRS of 120 ms or more suggests bundle branch block.
  • QTc is prolonged above about 440 ms in men and 460 ms in women.
  • A QTc over 500 ms raises the risk of torsades de pointes.
  • Each small box on ECG paper is 40 ms and each big box is 200 ms.
  • A normal adult ECG report shows normal sinus rhythm at 60 to 100 beats a minute with a normal axis (about −30° to +90°).

ECG Normal Values: FAQs

What is a normal PR interval?

120 to 200 ms in adults, which is 3 to 5 small boxes on standard ECG paper.

What is a normal QRS duration?

Under 100 ms. A QRS of 120 ms or more suggests a bundle branch block.

What is a normal QTc?

Up to about 440 ms in men and 460 ms in women. A QTc over 500 ms raises the risk of a dangerous rhythm.

What does a PR interval over 200 ms mean?

First-degree AV block, a delay in the signal passing through the AV node.

What causes a long QT?

Low potassium, magnesium or calcium, some medicines, ischaemia, very low body temperature and inherited long QT syndrome.

How do you calculate heart rate from an ECG?

On standard ECG paper running at 25 mm per second, divide 300 by the number of large squares between two R waves, or 1500 by the number of small squares. For example, 4 large squares means 300 / 4 = 75 beats a minute. If the rhythm is irregular, count the QRS complexes in a 6-second strip and multiply by 10.

What does a normal ECG report look like?

A normal adult ECG shows normal sinus rhythm (a P wave before every QRS), a heart rate of 60 to 100 beats a minute, a PR interval of 120 to 200 ms, a narrow QRS, a normal axis, and no ST elevation or depression.

What does WNL mean on an ECG report?

WNL means "within normal limits": the measured rate, intervals and axis fall inside the normal adult ranges. A doctor should still read the full tracing.
