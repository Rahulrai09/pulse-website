Electrical Safety Testing of Medical Equipment (IEC 62353)

A machine can work well and still leak current to a patient. Electrical safety tests find this before it causes harm. This guide covers the tests, the pass limits and how often to test.

PBy Pulse Team · Updated November 24, 2026 · 4 min read
Share

Hospital equipment is tested to IEC 62353, adopted in India as IS/IEC 62353. The main checks are protective earth resistance (300 mΩ or less), insulation, and leakage current. Equipment leakage must stay under 500 µA for Class I machines. Parts that touch the heart (type CF) must stay under 50 µA.

Why Test for Electrical Safety

Every mains-powered machine leaks a little current. Wear, damage or a bad repair can raise it. A patient connected to leads or probes can carry that current. Safety tests check that it stays within safe limits.

India's NHSRC maintenance guidelines say a qualified person should test each machine with an electrical safety analyser as part of maintenance.

The Standard: IEC 62353

IEC 62353 covers safety tests on medical electrical equipment. It is used before first use, during maintenance, after repair, and at regular checks. India adopted it as IS/IEC 62353. A newer edition came out in 2014. IEC 60601-1 is a different standard, used for type tests when a device is designed.

The Tests, in Order

  1. Visual check: look for damage to the case, cable, plug and leads.
  2. Protective earth resistance: checks the earth path on Class I machines. The test current is at least 200 mA.
  3. Insulation resistance: done at 500 V DC. It is optional, so ask the maker first.
  4. Leakage current: equipment leakage, and applied part leakage for BF and CF parts.
  5. Function check: confirm the machine still works as it should.

Pass Limits

TestLimit
Earth resistance, Class I machine with its power cable300 mΩ or less
Earth resistance, detachable power cable alone100 mΩ or less
Earth resistance, medical system500 mΩ or less
Equipment leakage, Class I (direct or differential method)500 µA
Equipment leakage, Class II (direct or differential method)100 µA
Applied part leakage, type BF5,000 µA
Applied part leakage, type CF50 µA
Applied part leakage, CF defibrillation paddles100 µA

Sources: IS/IEC 62353 and Rigel Medical's guide to IEC 62353. The alternative test method has higher equipment limits: 1,000 µA for Class I and 500 µA for Class II.

What B, BF and CF Mean

  • Type B: the part that touches the patient is linked to earth. Not for direct contact with the heart.
  • Type BF: a floating part with more protection than B. Not for direct contact with the heart.
  • Type CF: a floating part with the most protection. Suitable for direct contact with the heart.

The type is printed as a symbol on the machine and in its manual.

Extra Tests for Some Machines

The NHSRC guidelines list extra tests. Electrosurgical units need RF leakage and power output checks. Defibrillators need energy delivered, energy loss and battery checks.

How Often to Test

Follow the maker's manual. If it gives no interval, Rigel's guide suggests every 6 to 36 months, based on risk. We suggest testing at each preventive maintenance visit and after every repair. See our preventive maintenance schedule.

Keep the Results

Record each test result, the analyser used and the date in the machine's logbook. These records help with NABH. See our NABH equipment requirements guide.

Electrical Safety Testing: Key Facts

  • IEC 62353 is the standard for in-service safety tests; India uses IS/IEC 62353.
  • Earth resistance of a Class I machine with its cable must be 300 mΩ or less.
  • Equipment leakage limit is 500 µA for Class I and 100 µA for Class II.
  • Type CF parts, suitable for the heart, must leak under 50 µA.
  • Test before first use, after repair and at regular checks.

Electrical Safety Testing: FAQs

Which standard is used for electrical safety testing of medical equipment?

IEC 62353, adopted in India as IS/IEC 62353. It covers tests before use, during maintenance and after repair.

What is the earth resistance limit for medical equipment?

Under IEC 62353, a Class I machine with its power cable must be 300 mΩ or less. A detachable cable alone must be 100 mΩ or less.

What is the leakage current limit for medical equipment?

Equipment leakage must be under 500 µA for Class I and 100 µA for Class II by the direct or differential method.

What is the difference between BF and CF applied parts?

Both are floating parts. CF has the most protection and is suitable for direct contact with the heart. Its leakage limit is 50 µA, against 5,000 µA for BF.

How often should electrical safety testing be done?

Follow the maker. If no interval is given, one guide suggests 6 to 36 months by risk. We suggest testing at each service and after every repair.
