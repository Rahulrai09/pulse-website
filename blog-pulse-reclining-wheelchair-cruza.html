Pulse Reclining Power Wheelchair: Cruza

For users who spend long hours in a wheelchair, the ability to shift position without a full transfer isn't a luxury feature — it's a genuine comfort and pressure-relief need. Cruza is built specifically around that requirement.

What is the Pulse Cruza wheelchair? The Pulse Cruza is a reclining power wheelchair from Pulse's Rehabilitation range, built for patients who need extended rest support. Its electric recline adjusts up to 160 degrees, controlled with the same one-hand 360-degree joystick used to drive the chair.

Who Should Choose the Cruza

Cruza suits users who:

  • Spend extended periods in the wheelchair through the day
  • Need to adjust position periodically for comfort or pressure relief without a full transfer
  • Are in post-acute or longer-term rehabilitation where rest support matters as much as mobility
  • Want recline control that doesn't require a separate control panel or caregiver assistance

Daily Use of the Cruza

Cruza's recline function is operated through the same one-hand, 360-degree joystick used to drive the wheelchair — there's no separate recline control to learn. The electric recline adjusts up to 160 degrees, giving a genuine range between upright and near-flat rest position, controlled entirely by the user without needing to switch tools or ask for help.

Why Choose Cruza Over a Standard Model

A standard power wheelchair keeps the user in one fixed seating position all day. For anyone who needs to shift position for comfort, circulation, or pressure relief, that's a real limitation. Cruza's reclining function addresses this directly, without adding complexity to how the chair is operated day to day.

Key Facts at a Glance

  • The Pulse Cruza backrest reclines electrically up to 160 degrees, close to flat.
  • Its 24V 20Ah lithium-ion battery stores about 480 Wh.
  • Top speed is 6 km/h.
  • WHO's 2023 Wheelchair Provision Guidelines recommend a personal assessment, proper fitting, user training and follow-up for every wheelchair user.

Flying with the Cruza

Airlines care about how much energy a wheelchair battery stores, measured in watt-hours (Wh). You work it out as volts × amp-hours. The Cruza uses a 24V 20Ah lithium-ion battery, which is about 480 Wh. That is above the 300 Wh limit that airline safety rules set for a battery removed from a wheelchair. If the battery stays fitted in the chair there is no watt-hour limit, but you must get the airline's approval in advance.

Always tell your airline before you fly. Removed and spare batteries must go in the cabin with their terminals covered. You may carry one spare battery up to 300 Wh, or two spares up to 160 Wh each.
