What a Biochemistry Analyzer Does

A biochemistry (clinical chemistry) analyzer measures chemicals in blood, serum and urine. It mixes the sample with a reagent and reads the colour change with light at a set wavelength. The result gives values for tests such as blood sugar, liver function (LFT), kidney function (KFT), lipid profile, uric acid and electrolytes.

Semi-Automatic or Fully Automatic?

In a semi-automatic analyzer, a technician pipettes the sample and reagent by hand and the machine incubates and reads the result. It costs less and suits clinics and small labs. In a fully automatic analyzer, the machine pipettes, mixes, incubates and reads by itself, so staff can load samples and walk away. It handles many more tests an hour with fewer manual errors.

What Drives the Price

1. Level of automation and speed

Fully automatic analyzers with more tests per hour cost more, but save staff time on busy days.

2. Open or closed reagents

An open system accepts reagents from many brands, so you can buy on price and availability. A closed system works only with the maker's reagents.

3. Wavelengths, incubation and storage

More filter wavelengths and temperature options cover more tests. Larger result memory and quality control (QC) tools help with accreditation.

4. Connectivity

A link to your lab software or printer saves typing and reduces mistakes.

5. Running costs

Reagents, calibrators, controls, cuvettes and the lamp are ongoing costs. Over a few years, cost per test usually matters more than the machine price.

Biochemistry Analyzer Price in India: FAQs

How much does a biochemistry analyzer cost in India?

Bajaj Finserv's August 2026 guide puts semi-automatic analyzers at about Rs 40,000 to Rs 1.25 lakh, fully automatic analyzers at Rs 2.2 lakh to Rs 6 lakh and floor-standing analyzers at Rs 4 lakh to Rs 10 lakh.

What is the difference between semi-automatic and fully automatic biochemistry analyzers?

In a semi-automatic analyzer the technician pipettes sample and reagent by hand. A fully automatic analyzer pipettes, mixes, incubates and reads by itself, so it handles more tests with less staff time.

Which tests does a biochemistry analyzer run?

Common tests include blood sugar, liver function, kidney function, lipid profile, uric acid and other clinical chemistry tests, depending on the reagents used.

What is an open reagent system?

An open system accepts reagents from many brands, so the lab can buy reagents on price and availability. A closed system works only with the maker's reagents.

What should a lab check before buying?

Check daily test volume, cost per test, open or closed reagents, wavelengths, QC tools, connectivity, warranty and local service.
