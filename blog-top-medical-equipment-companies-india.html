Top Medical Equipment Companies in India (2026 Guide)

There is no one honest answer to "best medical equipment company in India". A stent maker and a hospital bed supplier do not compete for the same order. This guide sorts companies by what they make, so you compare each one with the right peers.

Quick answer: There is no single "best" medical equipment company in India, because companies lead in different categories. Meril leads in interventional cardiology, Poly Medicure in infusion consumables, Skanray in critical care and imaging, and Agappe and Transasia in lab diagnostics. Compare companies within the category you are buying, and check each one's CDSCO licence and ISO 13485 scope.

India's Medical Equipment Landscape in 2026

India used to buy most of its medical devices from abroad. Today, many are made here. By November 2025, the government counted over 4,000 licensed device makers in the country. Some make one small product. Others make whole ranges. That is why a useful list of top companies has to be sorted by type of product. A leader in heart stents and a leader in hospital beds are not the same kind of company, even if both show up in the same search.

How We Selected the Companies on This List

We did not make one ranked list. We grouped companies by what they really make, and looked at five things:

  • Who makes it — does the company make the product, or resell another brand?
  • Depth of range — how many products in one field, not just one star item?
  • Rules and quality — CDSCO licence, ISO 13485 scope and other key certificates.
  • Use and service — do hospitals use it, and is there a service team behind it?
  • Made in India — is it truly made here, or imported and relabelled?

The field has large Indian makers, small expert firms, global brands with plants in India, regional firms, new startups and makers that cover many fields. Any of them can belong on a top list. What matters is knowing which kind you are dealing with before you compare price or service.

Indian Manufacturers Versus Multinational Brands

Global brands often have a longer track record and a large service network. But they cost more, and local support can be slower. Indian makers often win on faster service, products built for Indian hospitals and a lower total cost. This varies a lot by maker and by product. Neither side is always better. The right choice depends on the product, its risk class and how easily you can get service where you are.

What Hospitals Should Check Before Selecting a Company

A known brand name is not enough for high-risk equipment. Before you pick a supplier, ask for:

  • CDSCO manufacturing or import licence details
  • The device's risk class (A, B, C or D)
  • ISO 13485 certificate and its exact scope
  • Product technical specifications
  • Trial or test data, where it applies
  • Install and handover papers
  • Warranty, AMC and CMC terms
  • Service schedule
  • Spare parts and how fast they will respond
  • Staff training, and how complaints are handled

India regulates medical devices under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940, and the Medical Devices Rules, 2017. There are four risk classes: Class A (low), Class B (low-moderate), Class C (moderate-high), and Class D (high risk). The higher the class, the more of these papers a real maker should have ready to share.

Why the Manufacturer-First Model Is Gaining Attention

More hospitals now buy straight from makers who cover many kinds of equipment. This saves them from using a different dealer for each department. Top Medical Equipment Companies in India (2026 Guide): FAQs

Is there an official "best medical equipment company" ranking in India?

No single authoritative ranking exists. Government and industry bodies publish licensing data and directories, not comparative rankings, which is why category-based evaluation is more useful than a single "best" list.

Are Indian-manufactured devices as reliable as imported ones?

It depends entirely on the specific manufacturer and product — some Indian manufacturers meet demanding international quality benchmarks, others don't yet have the track record. Country of origin alone isn't a reliable proxy for quality; check the documentation instead.

What's the difference between a manufacturer and a distributor in this list?

A manufacturer designs, builds, and holds regulatory responsibility for the product. A distributor resells another company's products. Some companies do both across different parts of their portfolio — always confirm which role applies to the specific product you're buying.
