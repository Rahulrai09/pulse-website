What Is EtCO2? Capnography Explained, With Normal Range

EtCO2 (end-tidal carbon dioxide) is the carbon dioxide in each breath out. Capnography shows it as a number and a waveform. This guide explains the normal range, what high and low values mean, and why it is used in operating theatres, ICUs and during CPR.

What Is EtCO2 and Capnography?

Your body makes a gas called carbon dioxide (CO2) and you breathe it out. EtCO2 is the CO2 level at the very end of a breath out, when the reading best matches the level in the lungs. Capnography shows this as a number and as a wave for every breath, usually on a patient monitor or ventilator.

EtCO2 Normal Range

EtCO2 readingWhat it usually means
35 to 45 mmHgNormal
Below 35 mmHgBreathing too much (hyperventilation), low CO2
Above 45 mmHgBreathing too little (hypoventilation), high CO2

These ranges come from a US emergency medicine journal review. Some people with long-term lung disease (COPD) live with higher values, sometimes above 60 mmHg.

Where EtCO2 Is Used

  • Checking a breathing tube: CO2 in the breath out shows the tube is in the windpipe, not the food pipe. Waveform capnography is described as the gold standard for confirming intubation.
  • Anaesthesia: the US anaesthesia society's rules (October 2025) say staff must check CO2 to confirm the tube is in place, and keep EtCO2 on while the tube or airway device is in.
  • Sedation: the same rules ask staff to watch breathed-out CO2 during deep sedation too, because sedative drugs can slow breathing and raise EtCO2.
  • ICU and ventilated patients: the trend helps staff spot breathing problems and set the ventilator.

EtCO2 During CPR

  • A falling EtCO2 during CPR can mean the person doing compressions is tiring, so rescuers should switch.
  • A sudden large rise can mean the heart has started again (return of circulation).
  • Values below 10 mmHg after long CPR are a bad sign.

EtCO2 vs SpO2

MeasuresCarbon dioxide in breath outOxygen carried in the blood

EtCO2 and Capnography: Key Facts

  • EtCO2 is the carbon dioxide at the end of each breath out.
  • Normal EtCO2 is 35 to 45 mmHg.
  • Below 35 suggests hyperventilation; above 45 suggests hypoventilation.
  • Waveform capnography is the gold standard to confirm a breathing tube is in the windpipe.
  • ASA standards require continual EtCO2 while a breathing tube or airway device is in place.

EtCO2 and Capnography: FAQs

What is the normal EtCO2 range?

35 to 45 mmHg. Below 35 suggests breathing too much; above 45 suggests breathing too little.

What is EtCO2 on a patient monitor?

End-tidal carbon dioxide: the CO2 level at the end of each breath out, shown as a number and a waveform (capnography).

Why is capnography used during anaesthesia?

To confirm a breathing tube is in the windpipe and to watch breathing throughout. US anaesthesia standards require it while a tube or airway device is in place.

What does EtCO2 show during CPR?

A falling value can mean compressions are weakening; a sudden large rise can mean the heart has restarted.

What is the difference between EtCO2 and SpO2?

EtCO2 measures carbon dioxide breathed out and shows ventilation. SpO2 measures oxygen in the blood.
