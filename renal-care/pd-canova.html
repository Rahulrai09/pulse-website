How AV Fistula Needles Are Used

Most patients on long-term haemodialysis have an arteriovenous (AV) fistula: a vein in the arm joined to an artery so it becomes large and strong enough for dialysis. At each session, two needles go into the fistula. The arterial needle takes blood to the machine and the venous needle returns the cleaned blood.

PD-Canova is supplied as a twin set, one arterial and one venous needle, with colour-coded wings so staff can tell the gauges apart quickly.

How to Choose the Right Gauge

  • 17G is the smallest of the three. It is often chosen for a new fistula that is still maturing, or for lower blood flow.
  • 16G is a common middle choice for established fistulas.
  • 15G is the largest bore. It allows higher blood flow, for mature fistulas and higher prescribed flows.
  • Back-eye needles have an extra side opening near the tip, which can help steady blood flow. PD-Canova comes with or without a back eye.
  • The final choice of gauge is always made by the treating nephrologist and dialysis team, based on the fistula and the prescription.
