How it works

How a Dialyser Works During Haemodialysis

A dialyser is the filter at the heart of haemodialysis. Inside it are thousands of thin hollow fibres made of a semi-permeable membrane. Blood flows through the inside of the fibres, and dialysate (a clean, balanced fluid made by the machine) flows around the outside in the opposite direction.

Waste such as urea and creatinine moves from the blood into the dialysate, because the dialysate has less of it. This is called diffusion. The machine also pulls extra water out of the blood across the same membrane. This is called ultrafiltration. Read how a dialysis machine works for a simple step-by-step guide.

Buyer checklist

What to Check Before Ordering Dialysers

Six things to confirm with your nephrologist and supplier before you place a dialyser order.

  • Membrane surface areaLarger surface areas clear more waste per session. Match the size to your patients and prescribed treatment.
  • Low-flux or high-fluxHigh-flux membranes let larger molecules and more water pass. Your nephrologist decides which suits each patient.
  • Single use or reuse policyConfirm the dialyser fits your unit's policy on reuse and reprocessing.
  • ConnectionsCheck that the dialyser fits the blood lines and dialysate connectors used on your machines.
  • Sterilisation and shelf lifeNote the sterilisation method and expiry date on each batch.
  • SupplyA busy unit uses at least one dialyser per session, so agree stock levels and delivery times with the supplier.
