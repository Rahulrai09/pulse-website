3 vs 6 vs 12 Channel: What the Number Means

A standard diagnostic ECG uses 12 leads: 12 views of the heart's electrical activity. The channel count is how many of those leads the machine prints side by side at one time.

  • 3 channel: prints the 12 leads in groups of three, usually as four rows plus a rhythm strip. Good for clinics doing a handful of ECGs a day.
  • 6 channel: prints six leads at a time, so a full report is faster and easier to read. A common choice for hospital wards and OPDs.
  • 12 channel: prints all 12 leads together on a single page, the fastest option and the one cardiologists usually prefer for busy emergency and cardiac units.

A good 3, 6 or 12 channel machine all acquire the 12 leads at the same moment. What changes is speed of printing, report layout, screen, storage and connectivity.

What Drives the Price of an ECG Machine

1. Channel count and print layout

More channels means a wider thermal printer and faster reports, which raises the price.

2. Interpretation software

Automatic measurement and interpretation gives the doctor a first reading of intervals and common abnormalities. It is a starting point, not a diagnosis, but it saves time in busy settings.

3. Screen, keyboard and storage

A larger colour touchscreen, an alphanumeric keyboard for patient details, and internal storage for hundreds of reports all add cost, and all matter more as daily volume grows.

4. Filters and protection

Digital filters for baseline drift, AC (mains) and EMG (muscle) interference give cleaner traces. Defibrillator protection is essential if the machine will be used in emergency or ICU settings.

5. Connectivity

USB or SD export and PC software let you save, review and share reports, and connect with a hospital information system.

6. Warranty, service and consumables

Two quotes for similar machines can differ in warranty length, cable and electrode bundles, and how fast a service engineer can reach you. See our guide on why after-sales service matters.

Costs to Budget Beyond the Purchase Price

  • Thermal paper: the main recurring cost; check the roll or Z-fold size the machine uses
  • Electrodes and gel: disposable tab electrodes or reusable suction and clamp electrodes
  • Patient cables: they wear out with daily use and are worth pricing up front
  • Annual maintenance: an AMC or CMC once the warranty ends

Which ECG Machine Should You Buy?

  1. Up to about 10 ECGs a day, one doctor: a 3-channel 12-lead machine
  2. Wards, OPDs and small hospitals: a 6-channel machine for faster, clearer reports
  3. Emergency, ICU and cardiology: a 12-channel machine with storage, keyboard and PC export

Whatever the size of your clinic or hospital, buy for the volume you expect in two to three years, not just today.

ECG Machine Price in India: FAQs

How much does an ECG machine cost in India?

Bajaj Finserv's August 2026 price list puts single-channel ECG machines at about Rs 18,600 to Rs 85,000, 3-channel machines at about Rs 22,400 to Rs 60,000, 6-channel machines at about Rs 25,000 to Rs 64,500, and 12-channel machines at about Rs 32,000 to Rs 1.3 lakh or more.

What is the difference between a 3, 6 and 12 channel ECG machine?

All three record the standard 12 leads. The channel count is how many leads are printed side by side at one time. A 3-channel machine prints 3 leads at a time, a 6-channel machine 6, and a 12-channel machine prints all 12 together, which gives the fastest and most complete printout.

Is a 3 channel ECG machine enough for a clinic?

For a general practice or small clinic doing a few ECGs a day, a 3-channel 12-lead machine is usually enough. Hospitals, emergency rooms and cardiology departments that do many ECGs a day usually choose a 6 or 12 channel machine for speed and storage.

Does a 3 channel ECG machine record all 12 leads?

Yes, if it is a 3-channel 12-lead machine. It acquires all 12 leads at the same time and prints them in groups of three. The Pulse 3-Channel ECG Machine, for example, uses simultaneous 12-lead acquisition.

What should I check before buying an ECG machine?

Check the lead and channel count, automatic measurement and interpretation, filters for baseline drift and AC and muscle interference, defibrillator protection, battery backup, storage and PC export, paper and electrode costs, warranty and local service.

What are the running costs of an ECG machine?

The main running costs are thermal recording paper, electrodes (disposable or reusable), electrode gel, patient cables that wear out, and an annual maintenance contract once the warranty ends.
