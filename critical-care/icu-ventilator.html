How to Choose an ICU Ventilator

An ICU ventilator breathes for patients who cannot breathe well enough on their own. The Pulse ICU ventilator covers neonates, children and adults on one machine, with invasive ventilation (through a breathing tube), non-invasive ventilation (through a mask) and high-flow oxygen therapy. Using one model across age groups means one set of training, spares and service.

Modes in simple words: in volume control (VCV) the machine gives a set amount of air with each breath; in pressure control (PCV) it pushes air up to a set pressure; SIMV mixes machine breaths with the patient's own; CPAP/PSV supports a patient who is breathing but needs help, and is often used while weaning. What to Check Before Buying an ICU Ventilator

  • Patient range. Confirm neonatal, paediatric and adult use if one model must serve all ICUs.
  • Modes. Match the invasive and non-invasive modes to how your intensivists work.
  • Gas supply. A turbine ventilator like this one makes its own airflow, so it does not need a compressed air supply. It still needs oxygen.
  • Battery backup. Essential for transfers and power cuts.
  • Consumables. Price circuits, filters, flow sensors and masks.
  • Training and service. Ask for on-site training and a clear service response plan.

What We Actually Checked Before Finalizing This Ventilator

A ventilator is not a product you buy on specs alone. Before this one went into our lineup, we looked hard at two things hospitals rarely ask about upfront: how it behaves on unstable mains power, and how fast a service engineer can reach it if something goes wrong. Indian ICUs do not always run on clean, constant power, so battery backup time and the way the unit handles voltage swings matter as much as the ventilation modes it supports.

On the clinical side, we paid attention to alarm behavior under real conditions, not demo conditions. A ventilator that triggers false alarms during routine suction or patient movement trains staff to ignore warnings, which is dangerous. This one is tuned to flag genuine issues like high pressure or disconnection without nagging the team over normal care activity. It supports the standard adult and pediatric modes ICUs expect, with a straightforward interface that does not require a long training cycle for new staff.
