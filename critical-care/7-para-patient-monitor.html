What Do the Parameters on a Patient Monitor Mean?

"Para" is short for parameter: one body sign the monitor measures. A 5-para monitor measures the five core signs: ECG (heart rhythm), SpO₂ (oxygen in the blood), NIBP (blood pressure with a cuff), respiration (breathing rate) and temperature. Pulse rate comes with the ECG and SpO₂.

A 7-para monitor adds two more. On the Pulse 7-para monitor these are EtCO₂ (carbon dioxide in each breath out, a key check for ventilated patients) and cardiac output, with IBP (blood pressure measured through an arterial line) as an option.

What to Check Before Buying Patient Monitors

  • Parameters per ward. Map each bed to the signs it needs before choosing 5-para or 7-para.
  • Patient types. Check NIBP cuffs and SpO₂ sensors for adults, children and newborns.
  • Central monitoring. If nurses watch many beds from one station, confirm networking support.
  • Alarms. Clear audio and visual alarms with adjustable limits reduce missed events.
  • Battery. Needed for patient transfers and power cuts.
  • Accessories. Price cables, cuffs and sensors too; they wear out and need replacing.

When Two More Parameters Change the Clinical Picture

A 7-parameter monitor adds invasive blood pressure and EtCO2 on top of the standard ECG, SpO2, NIBP, temperature, and respiration set. In a cath lab, OT, or high-dependency ICU, those two extra readings are often the ones that change a clinical decision in real time, which is why most hospitals running these settings choose this over a basic 5-parameter unit.

It is built to sit inside a hospital's existing monitoring network rather than as a standalone box: central monitoring station compatibility and printer output are both included, so a reading taken at the bedside is also visible to the team at the nurse's station. For departments upgrading from 5-parameter monitors, this is usually the natural next step rather than a full workflow change.
