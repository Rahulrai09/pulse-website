Refurbished vs New Medical Equipment in India: Cost, Rules and Risks

Refurbished machines can cut the price of big imaging machines such as CT and MRI by half or more. But India has strict, changing rules on used imports, and service and parts can be hard to get. This guide compares the two.

What Is Refurbished Medical Equipment?

Refurbished (or pre-owned) equipment is a used machine that has been checked, repaired, cleaned and tested, and then sold again. Sometimes the maker does this, and sometimes another firm. It is most common for costly imaging such as CT, MRI and X-ray, where a new machine can cost crores.

The Rules in India (2026)

  • No licence route yet: the device rules of 2017 treat new and used devices the same. In January 2025 the regulator (CDSCO) said used devices cannot be imported for sale until the rules give a way to license them.
  • Environment ministry route: used machines can still come in under the waste rules. This needs environment ministry clearance, CDSCO input and a DGFT import permit.
  • Only 38 device types: the list was cut from 50 to 38 in October 2024. It covers CT, MRI, mammography, radiotherapy, surgical robots, ultrasound, X-ray and some lab and ICU devices.
  • Conditions: the model must still be in use in the country it comes from, must not contain hazardous material, and must have at least 7 years of life left.
  • New policy pending: a government committee has worked on import rules for about a year, with no final rules yet.

Checklist Before Buying Refurbished

  • Ask who refurbished it: the maker, its partner or another firm.
  • Get the year it was made, hours used and service history in writing.
  • Check that the model is still supported for parts and software.
  • Ask for the import papers for imported machines.
  • Put the warranty, uptime and service terms in the contract.
  • Compare the total cost over 5 to 7 years, with service, not just the price.

When New Makes More Sense

Most of the 38 allowed types are costly imaging and surgical systems. For everyday equipment such as ICU beds, monitors, ventilators, infusion pumps, dialysis machines and lab analysers, buying new gives you full warranty, local service, parts and current software, without import rules to worry about. Refurbished vs New Medical Equipment: Key Facts

  • A 1.5T MRI costs about Rs 4 crore to Rs 8 crore new and Rs 1 crore to Rs 3.5 crore refurbished (trade report, September 2026).
  • Only 38 high-end device types may be imported used in India, under the environment ministry's hazardous waste rules.
  • Imported used equipment must have at least 7 years of remaining life and must not be phased out abroad.
  • CDSCO said in January 2025 that refurbished devices cannot be imported for sale without a licensing provision.
  • A government committee is still drafting rules for refurbished medical devices.

Refurbished vs New Medical Equipment: FAQs

Is refurbished medical equipment legal in India?

Used equipment can be imported only for 38 listed high-end device types, with environment ministry clearance, CDSCO input and DGFT authorisation. CDSCO has said refurbished devices cannot be imported for sale without a licensing provision, and new rules are still being drafted.

How much cheaper is refurbished medical equipment?

For imaging it can be much cheaper. A 1.5T MRI costs about Rs 1 crore to Rs 3.5 crore refurbished versus Rs 4 crore to Rs 8 crore new, according to a September 2026 trade report.

Which refurbished medical devices can be imported into India?

A list of 38 high-end types, including CT, MRI, mammography, radiotherapy, surgical robots, ultrasound, X-ray and some lab and critical care devices. Each must have at least 7 years of remaining life.

What should I check before buying refurbished equipment?

Who refurbished it, year of make and usage, service history, parts and software support, import papers, and warranty and service terms. Compare the total cost over 5 to 7 years.

When does new equipment make more sense than refurbished?

For everyday equipment such as monitors, ventilators, ICU beds and dialysis machines, which are mostly outside the list of 38 allowed used imports, new machines give full warranty, local service, parts and current software.
