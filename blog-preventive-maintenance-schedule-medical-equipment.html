Preventive Maintenance Schedule for Hospital Equipment in India

Equipment that is serviced on time breaks down less and reads true. This guide shows how often India's NHSRC rules say to service common hospital machines, what records to keep, and what uptime to aim for.

What Preventive Maintenance and Calibration Mean

Preventive maintenance (PM) is servicing done at set times, or as the maker advises, so a machine is less likely to break down. Calibration checks that a machine reads correctly. It is tested against a known standard.

Both terms come from the Guidelines on Equipment Maintenance, Calibration and Testing. These are from the National Health Systems Resource Centre (NHSRC), which advises India's health ministry.

The NHSRC rules list how many service visits each machine needs a year. Common items:

Start With Critical Equipment

The ministry lists some machines as critical and lifesaving. These include ventilators, defibrillators, infusion pumps, anaesthesia machines, ECG machines, baby warmers, X-ray, ultrasound and lab analysers. We suggest these go first on your service plan. Keep a backup ready for the days they are away.

Uptime Targets

Government hospitals aim for uptime of 95% in district hospitals, 90% in community health centres and 80% in primary health centres. Uptime is the share of machines found working when checked.

Records to Keep

  • Master list: ID, name, make, model, serial number, install date, warranty and AMC or CMC end dates, and status.
  • Logbook for each machine: service dates, the engineer's notes, when it broke and when it was fixed, and the head of department's sign-off.
  • Yearly check: which items work, which do not, which can be fixed and which are not worth fixing.

These records also help with NABH. See our NABH medical equipment requirements guide.

Electrical Safety Testing

At each service, a trained person should test the machine for electrical safety. The test checks power use, insulation and any stray current that could reach the patient. Some machines need extra tests. For example, defibrillators are checked for the energy they deliver and their battery. See our full electrical safety testing guide.

In-House Team, AMC or CMC?

An in-house engineer can often look after simple machines. Complex machines usually need the maker or an AMC or CMC contract. Compare the costs in our after-sales service guide.

Preventive Maintenance Schedule: Key Facts

  • NHSRC rules set how many service visits each machine needs a year.
  • Ventilators, monitors, pumps, autoclaves and dialysis machines get 4 visits a year.
  • Defibrillators, syringe pumps and ECG machines get 2 visits a year.
  • Uptime targets are 95%, 90% and 80% for district, CHC and PHC level.
  • Keep a master list and a logbook for every machine.

Preventive Maintenance Schedule: FAQs

How often should a ventilator get preventive maintenance?

NHSRC rules list 4 service visits a year for ventilators, about once every three months.

How often should an ECG machine be calibrated?

The NHSRC schedule lists 2 visits a year for ECG machines, about every six months.

What is the difference between preventive maintenance and calibration?

Preventive maintenance is planned servicing to stop breakdowns. Calibration checks that the machine reads correctly.

What equipment uptime should a hospital target?

Government targets are 95% in district hospitals, 90% in community health centres and 80% in primary health centres.

What records are needed for equipment maintenance?

A master list of all machines, a logbook for each one, and a yearly check of what works and what does not.
