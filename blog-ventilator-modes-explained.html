Ventilator Modes Explained: AC, SIMV, PSV, PRVC and More

A ventilator mode is the set of rules for how the machine gives each breath. This guide explains the common ICU modes in plain words, when each one is used, and what the main settings such as FiO2 and PEEP mean.

A ventilator mode decides who starts each breath and what the machine controls. In volume control the machine gives a set amount of air. In pressure control it pushes to a set pressure. The common ICU modes are assist control (AC), SIMV, pressure support (PSV) and PRVC. AC gives full support for every breath. PSV only helps breaths the patient starts.

What Is a Ventilator Mode?

Every breath from a ventilator follows three rules. What starts the breath, what the machine controls during it, and what ends it. A mode is a fixed set of these rules.

  • Start (trigger): the patient starts the breath, or the machine starts it on a timer when the patient does not breathe in time.
  • Control: the machine either sets the amount of air (volume) or the push (pressure).
  • End (cycle): the breath stops at a set volume, a set time or when the air flow drops.

The machine senses the patient's effort as a small drop in pressure or a change in flow in the tubing. Source: Walter et al., Southern Medical Journal, 2018.

PRVC goes by other names on different machines, such as VC+ or adaptive pressure ventilation. The name on the screen can differ, but the idea is the same.

AC vs SIMV: The Key Difference

In AC, every breath gets full support, even the extra breaths the patient starts. In SIMV, only the set number of breaths get full support. Any extra breaths are the patient's own effort, unless pressure support is added to them.

So a patient who breathes fast on AC gets many full breaths. On SIMV, the same patient does more of the work.

Main Ventilator Settings in Simple Words

  • Tidal volume: the size of each breath. For lung protection, OpenAnesthesia lists 6 mL per kg of predicted body weight.
  • Rate: the number of set breaths per minute.
  • PEEP: a small pressure kept in the lungs at the end of each breath, so they do not collapse. A common start is 5 cm H2O.
  • FiO2: the share of oxygen in each breath, from 21% (room air) to 100%.
  • Pressure support: the extra push added to breaths the patient starts.

What Is FiO2 on a Ventilator?

FiO2 means fraction of inspired oxygen. Room air is 21% oxygen. A ventilator can give anything from 21% to 100%, shown as 0.21 to 1.00 (eCampusOntario).

Teams often start at a high FiO2 when a patient first goes on the ventilator, then bring it down. High FiO2 is linked to oxygen toxicity, though it is generally thought safe for a short time. The University of Iowa course notes that a goal of FiO2 at or below 50% is sometimes used, with PEEP raised to help (University of Iowa).

Which Modes Are Used for Weaning?

Weaning means helping the patient breathe on their own again. Long ventilation raises the risk of weak muscles, lung injury and pneumonia, so teams test readiness early.

OpenAnesthesia describes a daily spontaneous breathing trial: about 30 minutes on low pressure support with little sedation, while staff watch oxygen and blood pressure. A rapid shallow breathing index (breathing rate divided by tidal volume) below 105 suggests the patient may be ready to come off (OpenAnesthesia).

Ventilator Modes: Key Facts

  • A mode sets what starts each breath, what is controlled and what ends it.
  • Volume control sets breath size; pressure control sets the push.
  • AC gives full support to every breath; SIMV only to the set breaths.
  • Pressure support helps only breaths the patient starts, with no set rate.
  • Room air is 21% oxygen; a ventilator can give 21% to 100% FiO2.

Ventilator Modes: FAQs

What are the main modes of a ventilator?

The common ICU modes are assist control (AC), SIMV, pressure support (PSV), PRVC, CPAP and APRV. Each can use volume control or pressure control.

What is the difference between AC and SIMV?

In AC every breath gets full support, including breaths the patient starts. In SIMV only the set number of breaths get full support, and extra breaths are the patient's own.

What is FiO2 on a ventilator?

FiO2 is the share of oxygen in each breath. Room air is 21%, and a ventilator can give from 21% to 100%.

What is PEEP on a ventilator?

PEEP is a small pressure kept in the lungs at the end of each breath so they do not collapse. A common starting value is 5 cm H2O.

Which ventilator mode is used for weaning?

Pressure support is widely used for breathing trials, and SIMV is used for weaning in some ICUs. A typical trial is about 30 minutes on low pressure support.

What is PRVC mode?

PRVC stands for pressure-regulated volume control. The machine changes the pressure breath by breath to reach a set breath size.
