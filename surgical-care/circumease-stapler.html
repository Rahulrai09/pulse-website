Why CircumEase

5 Reasons Surgeons Choose CircumEase

Shorter Operating Time

In a 2024 study of 301 children, the stapler took about 10 minutes against about 21 minutes for traditional circumcision. Shorter cases help busy theatres and day-care lists.

Less Bleeding

The same study measured about 4 mL of blood loss with the stapler against about 10 mL with the traditional method. The stapler cuts and closes in one action.

A Standardised Technique

The device sets the cut line, so the result depends less on individual technique. This helps hospitals keep results consistent across surgeons.

Sterile and Single-Use

Each kit comes sterile and is used once, so there are no instruments to clean and sterilise between patients.

A Size for Every Age

Eight sizes from 12 mm to 34 mm, chosen with the included measuring scale, cover neonatal, paediatric and adult patients.

How CircumEase Is Used

The surgeon first measures the glans with the included scale and picks the matching size. The bell of the device sits under the foreskin, and the stapler is closed and fired once. In that single action it cuts the foreskin and closes the edge with staples, so separate stitches are not needed.

What to Check Before Ordering

  • Size mix. Order across the eight sizes to match the ages you treat; children and adults need very different sizes.
  • Training. Ask for hands-on training for each surgeon before first use.
  • Aftercare plan. Healing takes longer than with traditional surgery, so give families clear wound-care and follow-up advice.
  • Sterility and expiry. Check each kit is sealed and within date.
  • Documents. Ask for the regulatory documents for the product before you buy.
