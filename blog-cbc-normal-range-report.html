CBC Normal Range: How to Read Your Blood Count Report

A complete blood count (CBC) is one of the most common blood tests. This guide lists typical adult normal ranges for each value on the report, explains what high and low results can mean, and why your lab's range may be a little different.

PBy Pulse Team · Updated November 21, 2026 · 4 min read
Share

Typical adult CBC normal ranges are: haemoglobin 13 to 17 g/dL in men and about 11.5 to 15.5 g/dL in women, white cells about 4,000 to 10,000 per microlitre, and platelets 150,000 to 400,000 per microlitre. Ranges differ between labs, so always compare your result with the range printed on your own report.

What a CBC Measures

A CBC counts and sizes the cells in your blood: red cells, which carry oxygen; white cells, which fight infection; and platelets, which help blood clot. A machine called a haematology analyser does the counting. For prices, see our CBC test price guide.

What High and Low Results Can Mean

  • Low haemoglobin: anaemia. High haemoglobin can come from dehydration, smoking, lung or heart disease, or living at high altitude.
  • Low MCV (small red cells): often iron deficiency or thalassaemia. High MCV (large red cells): often vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, alcohol use, liver disease or an underactive thyroid.
  • High white cells: often infection, inflammation or stress. Very high or very low counts need a doctor's review.
  • High neutrophils: often a bacterial infection, injury or inflammation. High lymphocytes: often a viral infection.
  • High eosinophils: often allergy or a parasite infection.
  • Low platelets: serious bleeding is uncommon above 50,000/µL, but the risk rises sharply below 20,000/µL.

One abnormal value does not make a diagnosis. Your doctor reads the whole report with your symptoms.

Why Your Range May Differ

  • Each lab sets its range for its own machines and methods.
  • Ranges differ for men and women, children and adults, and in pregnancy.
  • Units differ. For example, platelets may show as 150 to 400 ×10³/µL instead of 150,000 to 400,000/µL.

A falsely low platelet count can also happen when platelets clump in the sample tube, so labs may repeat the test.

3-Part vs 5-Part Reports

A 3-part analyser splits white cells into three groups: lymphocytes, mid-sized cells and granulocytes. A 5-part analyser reports neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils and basophils separately. See our 3-part vs 5-part analyser guide.

CBC Normal Range: Key Facts

  • Typical adult haemoglobin is 13 to 17 g/dL in men and about 11.5 to 15.5 g/dL in women.
  • Typical adult white cell count is about 4,000 to 10,000 per microlitre.
  • Typical platelet count is 150,000 to 400,000 per microlitre.
  • Low MCV often points to iron deficiency; high MCV to B12 or folate deficiency.
  • Always compare results with the range on your own lab report.

CBC Normal Range: FAQs

What is the normal CBC range for adults?

Typically haemoglobin 13 to 17 g/dL in men and 11.5 to 15.5 g/dL in women, white cells 4,000 to 10,000 per microlitre and platelets 150,000 to 400,000 per microlitre. Ranges vary by lab.

What is a normal haemoglobin level?

About 13 to 17 g/dL for men and 11.5 to 15.5 g/dL for women, according to one US hospital guide. The WHO uses 13 to 18 and 12 to 16 g/dL.

What does a high white blood cell count mean?

Often an infection, inflammation or stress. Very high or very low counts need a doctor's review.

What does low MCV mean?

Small red cells. This often points to iron deficiency or thalassaemia.

Why is my lab's normal range different?

Each lab sets ranges for its own machines and methods, and ranges differ by sex, age and pregnancy. Use the range printed on your report.
