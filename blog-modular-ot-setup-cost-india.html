Modular OT Setup Cost in India: What's Included, What Isn't, and How NABH Specs Drive the Number

The number most hospital projects start with for a modular OT budget is wrong. Not because the vendor quoted badly, but because the quote covered a smaller slice of what "operating theatre" actually means than the hospital assumed. This is a plain breakdown of the real cost layers, grounded in current NABH HVAC guidelines and published design references.

Short answer: A properly specified 36 m² (~388 sq ft) modular OT typically runs INR 70 lakh to INR 1.05 crore for the room itself (civil shell + modular panels + HVAC + flooring + lighting), and another INR 40-80 lakh for equipment (OT table, lights, pendants, anaesthesia machine). Realistic all-in INR 1.1-1.85 crore per fully equipped OT, before medical gas pipeline, ancillary rooms, and the consumables float. For a 4-OT cluster, expect 600-800 m² of total suite space including shared ancillary areas.

Why the "Per OT" Number Is Misleading on Its Own

Hospital planning teams routinely ask vendors "what does an OT cost" and compare quotes side by side. The problem: different vendors answer slightly different versions of that question. One quote includes HVAC and HEPA, another excludes them. One includes the pendant system, another counts it as equipment. One assumes you already have the civil shell, another prices the entire build from empty floor. The number is useless without knowing which layers it covers.

The cleanest way to compare is to break the cost into four separate layers:

  1. Civil + shell: walls, floor slab, structural work, basic plumbing and electrical rough-in.
  2. Modular fit-out: prefabricated wall and ceiling panels, flooring, doors, viewing windows, integrated services.
  3. HVAC + HEPA: AHU, ducting, HEPA filters, pressure management, controls.
  4. Equipment: OT table, OT light, pendants, anaesthesia workstation, electrosurgical unit, suction, defibrillator.

When you compare quotes layer-by-layer rather than as a lump sum, the actual spread between vendors becomes clear.

What NABH Actually Requires (and Why This Drives Cost)

NABH's 2018 revised guidelines for OT air conditioning are the single biggest cost driver for the modular + HVAC layers. The specifications are not optional for NABH-accredited hospitals, and they shape nearly every design choice downstream:

SpecificationType A OT (super-specialty)Type B OT (general)
Typical useNeuro, orthopaedics (joint replacement), cardiothoracic, transplantOphthalmology, day-care surgery, general / basic specialties
Air changes per hour (minimum)20 total (4 fresh air minimum)20 total (4 fresh air minimum)

Relative humidity20-60% (55% ideal)20-60% (55% ideal)
Air velocity at table25-35 FPM (unidirectional, downward)25-35 FPM (unidirectional, downward)
Minimum positive pressure2.5 Pa above adjoining areas (always)2.5 Pa above adjoining areas (always)
HEPA requirementMandatory (99.97% at 0.3 microns, ceiling terminal)Optional
Air cleanliness at grilleISO Class 5 (Class 100), at restISO Class 6 (Class 1000), at rest
Window or split AC allowed?NoNo
Validation frequencyEvery 6 months (per ISO 14664)Every 6 months (per ISO 14664)

Three practical consequences of these specs:

  • Terminal HEPA filtration above the table is a hard requirement for Type A OTs, which locks in a specific ceiling architecture (laminar airflow array) and significantly raises both the installation and ongoing filter-replacement cost.
  • The 2.5 Pa positive pressure requirement is "always maintained," including non-operational hours. This means the HVAC runs 24/7, which has a measurable electricity and PM cost that must be budgeted beyond year one.
  • The ban on window/split ACs eliminates every low-cost retrofit shortcut. The AHU and ducting are not negotiable.

Ancillary Rooms — The Area Most Projects Underestimate

An OT doesn't exist in isolation. The ancillary rooms around it typically add 60-80% more floor area to the suite:

  • Scrub: one station per surgeon per OT (~2.5 m² each)
  • Pre-anaesthesia / induction: 9-12 m² per OT
  • Recovery (PACU): at least 2 bays per OT at 8-12 m² each
  • Sterile store: 6-10 m² per OT cluster
  • OT clean store: 4-6 m² per OT
  • Dirty utility: 6 m² per OT
  • Per cluster: anaesthesia store (8-12 m²), surgeon's lounge (12-18 m²), change/locker (12-20 m²), control desk (6-10 m²), family waiting (25-60 m²)

Realistic total suite area: ~180-220 m² for one OT; ~600-800 m² for a 4-OT cluster. For 4 or more OTs, a two-corridor (clean + dirty) layout is standard; for 1-3 OTs, a single corridor with pass-through boxes suffices.

What's Still NOT in That Number

  • Medical Gas Pipeline System (MGPS) — oxygen x2, N2O x1, air x2, vacuum x3, scavenging x1 per OT, plus central gas plant / manifolds. Add INR 8-20 lakh for the per-OT plumbing, more if the plant doesn't exist yet.
  • Ancillary room build-out — scrub, pre-op, PACU, stores. For a 4-OT cluster this adds roughly 400-600 m² of built space at a lower rate than the OTs themselves (perhaps INR 8,000-12,000 per sq ft), so another INR 35-70 lakh total.
  • UPS + DG backup capacity for OT loads. Often shared across the suite; budget INR 10-25 lakh for OT-dedicated UPS and transfer gear.
  • Consumables float for the first 3-6 months of operation — disposables, drapes, surgical kits, sterilisation consumables. Budget INR 5-15 lakh per OT per month at steady state; the opening float is usually 2-3x monthly spend.
  • Validation and commissioning — air quality, pressure, particle count, microbial sampling. INR 1-3 lakh per OT at commissioning, then recurring every 6 months per NABH.
  • Furniture + consumable storage units — often skipped in the main quote.
The vendor quote and the actual spend diverge not because anyone is being dishonest, but because the quote covers a defined scope and the hospital's budget needs to cover everything the quote excludes. Insist on the exclusions list in writing before signing.

Three Realistic Project Shapes

Small Hospital, Single OT (30-50 bed facility)

  • 1 Type B general OT, 25-30 m²
  • Pre-op, recovery (2 bays), scrub, sterile store, dirty utility
  • Total suite ~180-200 m²
  • All-in budget estimate: INR 1.3-2 crore (OT + equipment + ancillary + MGPS)

Mid-Size Hospital, 2-OT Suite (80-150 bed facility)

  • 1 Type A + 1 Type B OT, 36 m² each
  • Shared pre-op, 4-bay PACU, scrub stations, stores
  • Total suite ~350-450 m²
  • All-in budget estimate: INR 3-4.5 crore

Multi-Specialty Hospital, 4-OT Cluster

  • 1 cardiac/neuro Type A (50-55 m²) + 2 general Type A (36 m² each) + 1 day-care Type B (25 m²)
  • Two-corridor layout (clean + dirty), 8-bay PACU, full ancillary set
  • Total suite ~700-800 m²
  • All-in budget estimate: INR 7-11 crore

Questions to Ask Every Vendor Before Signing

  • Which NABH guideline revision is your HVAC design certified against? (2018 is current.)
  • Is terminal HEPA included for the Type A OT, or at AHU level only?
  • What is the pressure differential maintenance plan during non-operational hours? (Required by NABH.)
  • What is the recommended HEPA filter change interval, and the per-change cost?
  • What air quality / microbial validation is included at commissioning? What is the recurring 6-monthly validation cost?
  • What backup HVAC runs if the primary AHU goes down mid-surgery?
  • What electrical load does the completed OT draw at peak, and is UPS capacity included or separately specified?
  • Which items on the equipment list are excluded? Pendants? OT table motor? Anaesthesia machine?
  • What AMC/CMC coverage is offered on panels, HVAC, and pendants — and for how long?
  • What's the lead time from PO to commissioning? What delays the schedule most often?

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a single modular OT cost to set up in India?

A properly specified 36 m² modular OT typically runs INR 70 lakh to INR 1.05 crore for civil + modular panels + HVAC + flooring + lighting (excluding surgical equipment). Equipment — OT table, lights, pendants, anaesthesia machine, electrosurgical unit — adds another INR 40 to 80 lakh. Realistic all-in cost per fully equipped OT: INR 1.1 crore to INR 1.85 crore, before considering medical gas pipeline, ancillary rooms, and the surgical consumables float.

What are the NABH requirements for an operating theatre?

NABH's 2018 revised guidelines require at least 20 total air changes per hour (minimum 4 of them fresh air), temperature of 21°C ±3°C (18°C ±2°C for joint replacement), relative humidity 20-60%, minimum positive pressure of 2.5 Pa, and ISO Class 5 (Class 100) cleanliness with mandatory HEPA for Type A (super-specialty) OTs. Window and split ACs are explicitly not permitted in any OT, regardless of type.

Does a hospital need separate pre-op and recovery rooms per OT?

Yes. Design references recommend 9-12 m² for pre-anaesthesia / induction per OT and at least 2 recovery bays per OT at 8-12 m² each, plus sterile store, scrub, dirty utility, and change rooms. For a single OT the total suite footprint usually comes to 180-220 m²; a 4-OT cluster typically needs 600-800 m² including shared ancillary space.

What's the difference between a modular OT and a conventional OT?

A modular OT uses prefabricated metal or vinyl-laminated wall and ceiling panels with sealed joints, integrated services (gas, electrical, data), and designed-in HVAC and HEPA filtration. A conventional OT uses plastered walls with epoxy paint and retrofitted services. Modular setups are easier to clean, easier to certify (NABH, infection-control audits), and faster to install — but carry a higher upfront cost. The modular premium is roughly 20-40% over a well-built conventional OT, which many hospitals recover in reduced infection and audit remediation costs.

Cost figures are typical ranges from current published design references, not Pulse-specific or vendor-specific pricing.
