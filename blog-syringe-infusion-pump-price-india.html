Syringe Pump and Infusion Pump Price in India: A Buyer's Guide

Syringe and infusion pumps look similar but do different jobs, and prices range from about Rs 12,000 to over Rs 1.9 lakh. This guide explains the price bands, the difference between the two, and how many a ward or ICU needs.

How much does a syringe or infusion pump cost in India? Bajaj Finserv's 2026 guides put basic single-channel syringe pumps at about Rs 12,000 to Rs 35,000 and volumetric infusion pumps at about Rs 22,000 to Rs 96,000. Multi-channel and specialised pumps can cost Rs 50,000 to Rs 1.95 lakh or more. Accuracy, safety alarms, battery life and service decide the price.

Syringe Pump vs Infusion Pump: Which Do You Need?

  • Syringe pump: drives the plunger of a syringe, usually 5 to 60 ml. Best for small volumes that must be delivered very precisely, such as sedation, vasopressors, heparin or insulin.
  • Volumetric infusion pump: moves fluid from a bag or bottle through an IV set. Best for larger volumes: fluids, antibiotics, blood products and nutrition.

Most ICUs, NICUs and OTs need both. Wards often start with infusion pumps and add syringe pumps for high-risk drugs.

What Drives the Price

1. Accuracy and flow range

Tighter accuracy and a wider flow range (from 0.1 ml/h for neonates and potent drugs up to 1,500 ml/h for fluids) cost more, and matter most for high-risk medicines.

2. Safety features

Occlusion (blockage) detection with adjustable pressure levels, air-in-line detection, near-empty and end-of-infusion alarms, and KVO (keep vein open) all raise the price, and all reduce risk.

3. Drug library and modes

Higher-end pumps store drug names and limits and offer dose-based modes. Basic pumps run by rate, volume and time.

4. Battery and build

Longer battery life matters for patient transfers and power cuts. Compact, light pumps are easier to mount on poles and stack at the bedside.

5. Consumables and service

A volumetric pump that needs its own branded IV sets can cost far more to run than the purchase price suggests. Check this, and check how quickly service is available.

How Many Pumps Do You Need?

As a starting point, plan at least one syringe pump and one infusion pump per ICU bed, and add more for patients on several infusions at once. On general wards, base the number on how many patients receive continuous infusions. Keep a few spare pumps for breakdowns and transfers.

Syringe Pump and Infusion Pump Price in India: FAQs

How much does a syringe pump cost in India?

Bajaj Finserv's June 2026 guide puts basic single-channel syringe pumps at about Rs 12,000 to Rs 35,000, dual or multi-channel pumps at about Rs 30,000 to Rs 60,000 or more, and high-end or specialised pumps at about Rs 50,000 to Rs 1.95 lakh or more.

How much does an infusion pump cost in India?

Bajaj Finserv's July 2026 guide puts volumetric infusion pumps at about Rs 22,000 to Rs 96,000, syringe infusion pumps at about Rs 17,500 to Rs 75,000, and enteral feeding pumps at about Rs 70,000 to Rs 85,000.

What is the difference between a syringe pump and an infusion pump?

A syringe pump pushes the plunger of a syringe (usually up to 50 or 60 ml) for small, very precise volumes, such as sedation, inotropes or insulin. A volumetric infusion pump moves fluid from a bag or bottle through an IV set, for larger volumes such as fluids, antibiotics and nutrition.

Which pump does an ICU need?

Most ICUs use both: syringe pumps for small-volume, high-risk drugs that need precise flow, and volumetric infusion pumps for fluids and larger infusions. Critically ill patients often need several pumps running at the same time.

What should I check before buying a syringe or infusion pump?

Check flow-rate range and accuracy, compatible syringe sizes or IV sets, occlusion (pressure) detection, air-in-line detection for volumetric pumps, KVO and bolus functions, battery life, alarm quality, and local service.

Do infusion pumps need special IV sets?

Some volumetric pumps only work with the maker's own IV sets, which raises running costs. Others, like the Pulse Infusion Pump, accept standard single-use IV sets from various brands. Ask about this before you buy.
