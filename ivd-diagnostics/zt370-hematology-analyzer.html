Who This Analyzer Suits

The ZT370 suits busier labs and hospital labs that need speed: its double chamber runs up to 72 tests an hour and stores up to a million results. A 3-part analyzer covers most routine CBC work; labs that need eosinophils and basophils counted separately look at 5-part analyzers. Read 3-part vs 5-part hematology analyzers to compare.

What to Check Before Buying a Lab Analyzer

  • Daily sample count. Match throughput to your busiest hour, not the daily average.
  • Cost per test. Ask for reagent consumption and price per test; it matters more than the machine price over time.
  • Open or closed reagents. Open systems accept many reagent brands; closed (RFID) systems lock in quality but limit choice.
  • Quality control. Check the QC modes and calibration support for your accreditation needs.
  • Connectivity. Ask how results reach your lab software or printer.
  • Service and uptime. A lab cannot stop; confirm engineer response and backup plans.
