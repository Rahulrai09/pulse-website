Hospital Bed for Home in India: Buy or Rent, and Which Type

Bringing a parent home after surgery, or caring for someone who is bedridden? This guide shows what a home hospital bed costs to rent or buy, which type fits which patient, and how to set it up safely.

Types of Hospital Bed for Home

  • Semi-Fowler (1 function): only the back rest lifts, by a hand crank or a motor. Good for short recovery after surgery, eating and easier breathing.
  • Full Fowler (2 functions): the back rest and knee rest both lift. Better for longer stays in bed.
  • 3 function: adds height change, so carers can lift and turn the patient without bending as much.
  • Electric (remote): motors do the work. Best when the patient must change position often or wants to adjust the bed alone.
  • ICU type (5 functions): adds head-down and feet-down tilt. For complex care at home, usually on a doctor's advice.
  • Commode bed: has an opening for toilet use, for patients who cannot get out of bed.

Should You Rent or Buy?

Rent for short needs, such as weeks or a few months of recovery. You pay little up front, and the company services the bed. Buy when the need is long-term, because owning costs less over time.

Our estimate from the prices above: a manual semi-Fowler bed rented for 6 months costs about Rs 8,000, close to the price of buying one. A Fowler bed with an electric head rents for Rs 4,000 a month, so buying a Rs 32,600 to Rs 44,100 electric Fowler bed pays off after about 8 to 11 months. As a rough rule, we suggest renting for under 6 months and buying for a year or more.

What to Check Before You Choose

  • Room size: a hospital bed is longer and wider than a single bed. Measure first and leave space on both sides for carers.
  • Weight limit: many home beds are rated for about 120 kg. Ask for a stronger bed for heavier patients.
  • Height change: it protects the carer's back when lifting, turning or changing sheets.
  • Wheels: lockable wheels let you move the bed for cleaning and keep it still in use.
  • Mattress: patients who stay in bed most of the day need a pressure-relief mattress to help prevent bed sores. Turning the patient often also helps.
  • Power: electric beds need a socket close by. A manual crank is useful if power cuts are common.

Side Rail Safety

Side rails help prevent falls, but badly fitted rails can trap a person's neck, chest or limbs in the gaps. Check that:

  • the mattress fits snugly between the rails, with no wide gaps at the sides or ends;
  • the rails are made for that bed, fitted firmly and checked often;
  • a thick overlay mattress does not make the rails too low to work.

Ask the supplier whether the bed and rails meet the international medical bed standard, IEC 60601-2-52.

Is it better to rent or buy a hospital bed?

Which hospital bed is best for a bedridden patient at home?

A Fowler or 3 function bed with an electric back rest, height change, side rails and a pressure-relief mattress. Ask the patient's doctor which positions are needed.
