Types of Delivery Tables

  • Manual. Back-rest and positions set by hand with a ratchet or gas spring. Lowest cost, no power needed.
  • Hydraulic or telescopic. Height set with a foot pump; often has Trendelenburg tilt. Easier for staff and patients.
  • Electric. Motors set height and back section with a remote, so staff can adjust quickly during labour.

What Changes the Price of a Delivery Table?

  • Material. Stainless steel (SS) tops and frames cost more than mild steel (MS) but resist rust and cleaning chemicals.
  • Sections. Three-section tops with a sliding leg end make delivery and repair easier.
  • Positions. Trendelenburg tilt, lithotomy poles and leg holders add cost.
  • Accessories. Mattress, IV rod, hand grips, bowl and side rails may or may not be included.

What to Check Before Buying a Delivery Table

  • Manual, hydraulic or electric operation
  • SS or MS top and frame
  • Three-section top with sliding leg end
  • Trendelenburg tilt and lithotomy or leg holders
  • Mattress, IV rod, hand grips and bowl in the price
  • Castors with brakes, and warranty

Delivery Table Price in India: FAQs

How much does a delivery table cost in India?

Manual delivery tables list at about Rs 7,000 to Rs 45,000, hydraulic and telescopic tables at Rs 25,000 to Rs 60,000, and electric obstetric tables at Rs 96,000 to Rs 2 lakh (IndiaMART listings, October 2026).

What is the difference between a delivery table and a labour bed?

A delivery table has a short, sectioned top with a sliding or removable leg end for the birth. A labour bed (or LDR bed) is a full-length bed that converts for delivery, so the mother stays in one bed for labour, delivery and recovery.

Should a hospital buy a manual or hydraulic delivery table?

Manual tables cost less and need no power. Hydraulic tables let staff change height with a foot pump and usually tilt, which helps during difficult deliveries.

Is a stainless steel delivery table better?

Stainless steel resists rust and repeated cleaning better than mild steel, so it lasts longer in a busy labour room, but it costs more.

What other equipment does a labour room need?

Most labour rooms also need a baby warmer, suction, oxygen, a fetal monitor, an OT or examination light and an instrument trolley.
