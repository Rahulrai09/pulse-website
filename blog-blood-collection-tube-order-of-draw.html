Blood Collection Tubes: Colours, Uses and the Order of Draw

Each blood tube contains a different additive, and drawing them in the wrong order can carry additive from one tube into the next and spoil results. This guide explains the tube colours, which tests each is used for, and the standard order of draw.

PBy Pulse Team · Updated November 21, 2026 · 4 min read
Share

The standard order of draw is: 1. blood culture, 2. citrate (light blue), 3. serum (red or gold), 4. heparin (green), 5. EDTA (lavender), 6. fluoride (grey). Following this order stops additives from one tube contaminating the next. Tube cap colours can vary by maker, so go by the additive.

Why the Order Matters

Blood tubes contain additives that stop clotting or help it, or protect glucose. If blood carries a little additive from one tube into the next, results can be wrong. The Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) sets the order to prevent this, and it applies whether blood is drawn by syringe, tube holder or vacuum tubes.

The Order of Draw

A Tip for Clotting Tests

Plastic serum tubes with clot activator can affect clotting tests, so they must not be drawn before the citrate tube. Only blood culture tubes, glass plain tubes or plastic serum tubes without clot activator may go before it. Citrate tubes must also be filled to the line.

Good Practice

  • Gently invert tubes 6 to 8 times to mix, or as the maker advises. Do not shake.
  • When filling vacuum tubes from a syringe, use a transfer device rather than pushing hard on the plunger.
  • Fill tubes to the correct volume.
  • Label each tube at the bedside with the patient's details.

From Tube to Analyser

EDTA tubes go to the haematology analyser for a CBC. Serum, heparin and fluoride samples go to the biochemistry analyser for tests such as liver and kidney function and glucose. See our CBC normal range, how a biochemistry analyzer works and lab QC guides.

Blood Tube Order of Draw: Key Facts

  • Order of draw: blood culture, citrate, serum, heparin, EDTA, fluoride.
  • The order prevents additives carrying over from one tube to the next.
  • EDTA (lavender) tubes are used for CBC and HbA1c.
  • Fluoride (grey) tubes are used for glucose and lactate.
  • Citrate tubes must be filled to the line for accurate clotting tests.

Blood Tube Order of Draw: FAQs

What is the correct order of draw for blood tubes?

Blood culture, then citrate (light blue), serum (red or gold), heparin (green), EDTA (lavender) and fluoride (grey), as set by CLSI.

Why does the order of draw matter?

It stops additives from one tube carrying over into the next and changing test results.

Which tube is used for a CBC?

The EDTA tube, usually with a lavender or pink cap.

Which tube is used for blood glucose?

The fluoride oxalate tube, usually with a grey cap, which stops cells using up the glucose.

How many times should blood tubes be inverted?

Gently 6 to 8 times, or as the tube maker advises. Do not shake them.
