How to Prevent Bedsores (Pressure Ulcers) in Hospital and at Home

Bedsores form when pressure on the skin cuts off blood flow, usually over bony areas. This guide covers who is at risk, early signs to watch for, how often to change position, and the equipment and skin care that help prevent them.

PBy Pulse Team · Updated November 21, 2026 · 4 min read
Share

To prevent bedsores, change position often: UK NICE guidance says at least every 6 hours for adults at risk and at least every 4 hours for those at high risk. Use a pressure-redistributing mattress, check the skin every day, keep it clean and dry, and get help early if a red or dark patch does not fade when pressed.

What Are Bedsores?

Bedsores, also called pressure ulcers or pressure sores, are damage to the skin and the tissue under it. They usually form over bony parts such as the heels, elbows, hips and tailbone. They often develop slowly, but can appear within a few hours.

Who Is at Risk?

  • People who find it hard to move, including those confined to bed or a wheelchair.
  • People who are seriously ill in intensive care, or have just had surgery.
  • People who have had a pressure ulcer before.
  • People who are underweight, or have poor circulation, fragile skin or reduced feeling.
  • People with skin that is swollen, sweaty or broken.

Early Signs

  • A patch that stays discoloured when pressed: red on light skin, purple or blue on darker skin.
  • An area that feels warm, spongy or hard.
  • Pain or itching in one spot.

Discoloured patches can be harder to see on darker skin, so staff check by touch as well as sight.

These steps come from the UK NICE guideline on pressure ulcers.

At Home

  • Help the person change position regularly, day and night.
  • Use a suitable mattress and seat cushion.
  • Check the skin every day, especially over bony areas.
  • Encourage movement where possible, a balanced diet and no smoking.

When to Get Help Urgently

Get medical help quickly if the skin around a sore is hot, swollen or red, if there is pus, a high temperature, or pain that is severe or getting worse. These can be signs of serious infection.

Bedsore Prevention: Key Facts

  • Bedsores usually form over the heels, elbows, hips and tailbone.
  • NICE: change position at least every 6 hours if at risk, every 4 hours if at high risk.
  • NICE recommends a high-specification foam mattress for adults in hospital.
  • An early sign is a patch of skin that stays discoloured when pressed.
  • Hot, swollen skin, pus or fever around a sore needs urgent medical help.

Bedsore Prevention: FAQs

How often should a bedridden patient be turned?

UK NICE guidance says adults at risk should change position at least every 6 hours, and those at high risk at least every 4 hours.

What are the early signs of a bedsore?

A patch of skin that stays red, purple or blue when pressed, feels warm, spongy or hard, or is painful or itchy.

Where do bedsores usually form?

Over bony parts of the body, such as the heels, elbows, hips and tailbone.

Who is most at risk of bedsores?

People who cannot move easily, are seriously ill or have had surgery, have had a pressure ulcer before, or are underweight with fragile skin.

What mattress helps prevent bedsores?

NICE recommends a high-specification foam mattress for adults admitted to hospital and for high-risk adults in the community.
