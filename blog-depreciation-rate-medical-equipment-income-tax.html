Depreciation Rate on Medical Equipment Under Income Tax (2026)

Most hospital machines are written down at 15% a year for income tax. A short list of life-saving equipment gets 40%. This guide shows which is which, how the 180-day rule works, and how the books differ from the tax return.

PBy Pulse Team · Updated November 24, 2026 · 4 min read
Share

Under India's income tax rules, most medical machines fall in general plant and machinery at 15% a year on written down value. A fixed list of life-saving equipment gets 40%. It includes ventilators, haemodialysors, heart-lung machines, MRI, colour Doppler and surgical lasers. If a machine is used for less than 180 days in its first year, you claim half the rate that year.

Which Law Applies in 2026

The Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect on 1 April 2026. It applies from tax year 2026-27, as the Income Tax Department explains. Depreciation is now under section 33 of the new Act. The rates are in Appendix I of the Income-tax Rules, 2026. For equipment, the rates did not change from the old rules.

Depreciation Rates for Hospital Equipment

Tax depreciation is worked out on the written down value (WDV) of each block of assets. That is the cost, less the depreciation already claimed.

AssetRate a year (on WDV)
Most medical machines (general plant and machinery)15%
Life-saving medical equipment on the official list40%
Computers and software40%
Furniture and fittings, including electrical fittings10%

Source: Appendix I, Income-tax Rules, 2026.

The Life-Saving Equipment List (40%)

Only these items get 40%. The list is fixed:

  • Defibrillators for internal use, and pacemakers
  • Haemodialysors
  • Heart-lung machine
  • Cobalt therapy unit
  • Colour Doppler
  • SPECT gamma camera
  • Vascular angiography system, including digital subtraction angiography
  • Ventilator used with anaesthesia apparatus
  • MRI system
  • Surgical laser
  • Ventilators other than those used with anaesthesia
  • Gamma knife
  • Bone marrow transplant equipment
  • Fibreoptic endoscopes, such as bronchoscopes and gastroscopes
  • Laparoscope (single incision)

Machines That Usually Get 15%

Anything not on the list goes in general plant and machinery at 15%. Tax officers read the list strictly. In one appeal, the tribunal held that CT scan, ECG and patient monitoring systems are not on the list. They got 15%, not 40% (Indian Kanoon). In another, a holmium laser got 15% because the hospital could not show it was on the list (ITAT order).

So patient monitors, ECG machines, infusion pumps, hospital beds and lab analysers are usually at 15%. Beds are often treated as furniture at 10%. We suggest you confirm the block for each item with your chartered accountant.

The 180-Day Rule

If you buy a machine and use it for less than 180 days in that tax year, you get half the rate for that year. This is in section 33(4) of the Act. The 180 days count from the day you start using it, not the bill date. A machine first used on 1 November gets 151 days before 31 March, so it gets half the rate.

A Worked Example

Say a hospital buys a ventilator for ₹10,00,000 and starts using it on 1 November. These are example figures, not a price quote.

Tax yearRate usedDepreciationWDV at year end
Year 1 (used under 180 days)20% (half of 40%)₹2,00,000₹8,00,000
Year 240%₹3,20,000₹4,80,000
Year 340%₹1,92,000₹2,88,000

A patient monitor bought for ₹2,00,000 and used from 1 June would get the full 15% in year 1. That is ₹30,000.

Books vs Tax Return

Companies also show depreciation in their accounts under the Companies Act, 2013. That uses useful life, not tax rates. Schedule II gives 13 years for electrical machinery, X-ray and electrotherapy apparatus, and 15 years for other medical and surgical equipment (Income Tax Department rate tables). So the figure in your books and the figure in your tax return will differ.

Medical Equipment Depreciation: Key Facts

  • Most medical machines are depreciated at 15% a year on written down value.
  • A fixed list of life-saving equipment, including ventilators, gets 40%.
  • Patient monitors, ECG machines and CT were held not to be on the 40% list.
  • Machines used under 180 days in the first year get half the rate.
  • Companies Act books use useful life: 13 or 15 years for medical equipment.

Medical Equipment Depreciation: FAQs

What is the depreciation rate on medical equipment in India?

Most medical equipment is at 15% a year on written down value. Equipment on the life-saving list gets 40%.

Is a ventilator life-saving equipment for 40% depreciation?

Yes. Ventilators, both with and without anaesthesia apparatus, are on the life-saving list in Appendix I.

Do patient monitors and ECG machines get 40% depreciation?

Usually not. A tax tribunal held that ECG machines, patient monitoring systems and CT scans are not on the list, so 15% applied.

What is the 180-day rule for depreciation?

If a machine is put to use for less than 180 days in the year it is bought, only half the normal rate is allowed that year.

What is the useful life of medical equipment under the Companies Act?

Schedule II gives 13 years for electrical machinery, X-ray and electrotherapy apparatus, and 15 years for other medical and surgical equipment.
