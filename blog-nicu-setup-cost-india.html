NICU Levels in India

India plans newborn care at three levels. The level you build decides the equipment, space, staff and budget, so pick it first.

LevelWhereBedsWhat it treats
Level I: Newborn Stabilisation Unit (NBSU)CHCs, small hospitals4 to 6Stabilises sick or small babies and refers them on
Level II: Special Newborn Care Unit (SNCU)District hospitals, busy nursing homes12 or moreWarmth, oxygen, CPAP, phototherapy, IV fluids; no ventilators
Level III: NICUMedical colleges, large private hospitals16 or more (NNF)Ventilation, very small preterm babies, round-the-clock neonatologists

Most district hospitals and busy private maternity hospitals start with a Level II unit. A Level III NICU makes sense only with an attached delivery unit, enough sick babies, and neonatologists on duty day and night.

What a 12-Bed SNCU Costs

The Government of India’s Facility Based Newborn Care (FBNC) Operational Guidelines 2025 give a clear budget for a 12-bed unit:

  • Equipment: Rs 1 crore.
  • New building: Rs 1.3 crore (3,190 sq ft at about Rs 4,084 per sq ft, including a mothers’ area).
  • Major renovation instead: about half the building cost.
  • Running costs: Rs 14 lakh a year for consumables and equipment upkeep, plus Rs 1 lakh for records. Staff salaries are extra.

Private hospitals often spend less on the building and choose their own brands, but the government figure is a solid starting point for planning.

Space and Staff Rules

  • SNCU space: 120 sq ft per bed, or 170 sq ft per bed with CPAP (FBNC 2025).
  • Level III space: 150 sq ft per bed, including 50 sq ft of support area (NNF).
  • SNCU nurses: one nurse per 4 beds per shift, which means 16 nurses for 12 beds; CPAP beds need one nurse per 3.
  • Level III nurses: one per 2 ventilated beds and one per 4 other beds per shift, plus a 20% reserve.
  • Doctors: a paediatrician or FBNC-trained doctor every shift in an SNCU; a full-time neonatology in-charge and two senior doctors per shift in a Level III unit.

How to Plan and Save

  • Start at Level II and add ventilated beds later, once you have the staff and the cases.
  • Put CPAP in at least a third of the beds; it keeps many babies off a ventilator.
  • Plan the medical gas pipeline (oxygen, air and suction at every bed) before the civil work.
  • Choose servo-controlled warmers; they hold the baby’s skin temperature steady and are what NNF asks for.
  • Ask for a service contract and spare parts for every warmer, pump and ventilator.

NICU Setup Cost in India: FAQs

How much does it cost to set up a NICU in India?

A 12-bed SNCU is budgeted by the Government of India at Rs 1 crore for equipment and Rs 1.3 crore for new building work (FBNC guidelines, 2025). A private 16-bed Level III NICU needs about Rs 52 lakh to Rs 1.56 crore of core equipment, plus about Rs 1 crore of building work, by our estimate.

What is the difference between NICU and SNCU?

An SNCU (Level II) gives warmth, oxygen, CPAP, phototherapy and IV fluids but no mechanical ventilation. A NICU (Level III) adds ventilators and round-the-clock neonatologists. The government guideline says simply adding ventilators to an SNCU does not make it a NICU.

How much space does a NICU need per bed?

Indian guidelines give 120 sq ft per bed for an SNCU, 170 sq ft per bed if the bed has CPAP, and NNF Level III standards ask for 150 sq ft per bed. Each figure includes about 50 to 70 sq ft of support area such as the nursing station and store.

How many nurses does a NICU need?

NNF Level III standards ask for one nurse for every 2 ventilated babies and one for every 4 other babies in each shift, plus a 20% reserve. A unit with 6 ventilated and 10 other beds needs about 22 nurses.

What equipment is needed for a NICU?

The core list is a radiant warmer or incubator for every bed, ventilators and CPAP, phototherapy (one unit per 2 beds), monitors or pulse oximeters at every bed, syringe and infusion pumps, suction, resuscitation kits, a blood gas analyser and portable X-ray.
