When Is Dialysis Needed? Indications, eGFR and Creatinine Level

Many families ask at what creatinine level dialysis starts. No single number decides it. This guide explains the stages of kidney disease, how doctors decide, the warning signs and the emergencies.

Dialysis is usually needed at kidney failure, when the eGFR stays below 15 and the kidneys have lost about 85 to 90% of their function, and symptoms or blood results show the body can no longer cope. There is no single creatinine level that decides it. Emergency dialysis is needed for very high potassium, severe acid build-up, fluid in the lungs, some poisonings, or severe uraemia.

Dialysis Starts at Kidney Failure

The US National Kidney Foundation (NKF) defines kidney failure as an eGFR below 15 for 3 months or more. At this stage 85 to 90% of kidney function is gone. eGFR is an estimate of how well the kidneys filter. It is worked out from a creatinine blood test, age and sex.

Reaching an eGFR of 15 does not mean dialysis starts that day. It is the point to plan and get ready.

Stages of Kidney Disease

The NKF groups chronic kidney disease into stages by eGFR:

StageeGFRWhat it means
190 or moreKidney damage with normal filtering
260 – 89Mild loss of kidney function
3a45 – 59Mild to moderate loss
3b30 – 44Moderate to severe loss
415 – 29Severe loss of kidney function
5Below 15Kidney failure

Most people who need dialysis are in stage 5.

How Doctors Decide When to Start

The UK guideline NICE NG107 says to consider starting dialysis when:

  • symptoms of kidney failure (uraemia) affect daily life, or
  • blood results become dangerous, or
  • fluid build-up cannot be controlled, or
  • the eGFR reaches about 5 to 7 if there are no symptoms.

NICE adds that the decision should be made jointly by the patient, family and the kidney team. It found that starting at an eGFR of about 5 to 7 without symptoms caused no overall harm or benefit compared with starting earlier. NICE also warns that some symptoms may come from other illnesses, so these should be checked first.

Is There a Creatinine Level for Dialysis?

No. There is no fixed creatinine number at which everyone starts dialysis. Creatinine depends on muscle mass, age and sex, so the same level can mean different things in different people. Doctors use the eGFR together with symptoms, potassium, acid levels and fluid. NICE calls the timing a complex decision that takes all of these into account. We suggest asking your nephrologist what eGFR and symptoms they will watch for in your case.

Symptoms That Dialysis May Be Needed

As kidney function falls, people may (Kaiser Permanente):

  • feel very tired
  • feel sick or vomit
  • have swelling from fluid build-up
  • lose appetite and weight
  • have trouble thinking
  • notice a metallic taste in the mouth
  • have severe itching

Tell your kidney doctor about any of these. The NKF says the most common reason to start dialysis is fluid build-up, such as swelling that is hard to control.

Emergency Reasons for Dialysis (AEIOU)

Doctors use the memory aid AEIOU for urgent dialysis, usually in hospital (UCSF Hospital Handbook):

LetterMeansExample
AAcidosisSevere acid build-up in the blood (pH below about 7.2) that does not improve with treatment
EElectrolytesVery high potassium, usually above 6.5, or lower with ECG changes
IIngestionsSome poisonings, such as lithium or ethylene glycol
OOverloadFluid in the lungs that does not respond to medicines
UUraemiaConfusion, fits, inflammation around the heart or bleeding from kidney failure

These can happen in sudden kidney injury as well as in long-term kidney disease. High potassium can show up on an ECG; see our ECG normal values guide.

Tests Your Doctor Will Watch

  • eGFR: worked out from blood creatinine. See our KFT test price guide.
  • Potassium: high levels can upset the heart rhythm.
  • Acid level: shows whether acid is building up in the blood.
  • Fluid: weight, swelling and breathing.
  • Symptoms: appetite, energy, itching and thinking.

We suggest keeping a simple record of each result and your weight, and taking it to every visit.

Your Options at Kidney Failure

OptionWhat it means
HaemodialysisBlood is cleaned by a machine, usually in a centre 3 times a week for about 4 hours each time (NKF). It can also be learned for home.
Peritoneal dialysisThe lining of the belly is used as a filter, usually at home.
Kidney transplantSometimes done before dialysis is needed, if kidney failure is getting worse slowly (NKF).
Conservative careTreating symptoms without dialysis. NICE covers it as a care option.

When Is Dialysis Needed: Key Facts

  • Kidney failure means an eGFR below 15 for 3 months or more, with 85 to 90% of kidney function gone (NKF).
  • No single creatinine level decides when dialysis starts.
  • NICE: start when symptoms affect daily life, blood results are dangerous, fluid cannot be controlled, or eGFR is about 5 to 7 without symptoms.
  • Urgent dialysis reasons are remembered as AEIOU: acidosis, electrolytes, ingestions, overload, uraemia.
  • The decision to start is made jointly by the patient, family and kidney team (NICE).

When Is Dialysis Needed: FAQs

At what creatinine level is dialysis needed?

There is no single creatinine level. Doctors look at the eGFR (worked out from creatinine, age and sex) together with symptoms, potassium, acid levels and fluid. Dialysis usually starts at kidney failure, when eGFR is below 15, once symptoms or dangerous blood results appear.

At what eGFR does dialysis start?

Kidney failure is an eGFR below 15. The NICE guideline says to consider starting when symptoms affect daily life or blood results or fluid are uncontrolled, or at an eGFR of about 5 to 7 if there are no symptoms.

What is stage 5 kidney disease?

Stage 5 is kidney failure: an eGFR below 15 for 3 months or more, according to the National Kidney Foundation. It is the stage at which dialysis or a transplant is usually needed.

What are the indications for dialysis?

Long-term: kidney failure with symptoms of uraemia, uncontrolled fluid or dangerous blood results. Urgent: AEIOU, meaning severe acidosis, very high potassium, some poisonings, fluid in the lungs that does not respond to medicines, and severe uraemia.

What are the signs that you need dialysis?

Common signs are tiredness, nausea or vomiting, swelling, loss of appetite and weight, trouble thinking, a metallic taste and severe itching. Tell your kidney doctor about any of these.

Can dialysis be avoided?

Some people can have a kidney transplant before dialysis is needed, and some choose conservative care without dialysis. We suggest asking your nephrologist how to slow kidney disease and which options suit you.

Is dialysis needed in sudden kidney failure?

Sometimes. In sudden kidney injury, urgent dialysis may be needed for very high potassium, severe acid build-up, fluid in the lungs that does not respond to medicines, some poisonings or severe uraemia.
