Electric Wheelchair Battery Care: How to Charge and Make It Last

The battery is the part of an electric wheelchair that wears out first, and how you charge and store it makes a big difference. This guide covers safe charging, why heat matters, how to store a chair you are not using, and when a battery needs replacing.

PBy Pulse Team · Updated November 21, 2026 · 4 min read
Share

To make a lithium-ion wheelchair battery last, charge it after use and avoid running it flat. Partial top-ups are fine. Keep the battery out of heat (above 30°C ages it faster), use only the charger supplied, and charge indoors on a hard surface. If storing the chair, leave the battery part-charged in a cool, dry place.

Lithium-Ion vs Gel Batteries

Older and some larger power chairs use sealed lead-acid or gel batteries. Many light folding chairs use lithium-ion. The care is a little different, so always check which type you have and follow the maker's manual. Pulse electric wheelchairs use 24V lithium-ion batteries.

How to Charge

  • Use only the supplied charger. A different charger may not match the battery.
  • Switch the chair off before connecting the charger, and do not drive while charging.
  • Charge indoors on a hard surface in a room with good airflow. Do not cover the charger, as it gets warm.
  • Keep the charger away from water, direct heat and sunlight.
  • Switch off at the mains before plugging in or unplugging the charger, and pull the plug, not the cable.

These steps come from an NHS wheelchair service battery guide.

When to Charge a Lithium-Ion Battery

Lithium-ion batteries have no "memory", so you do not need to run them flat before charging. Battery testing shows that shallow use lasts much longer than full drains: about 300 cycles at 100% depth of discharge, against about 2,000 cycles at 20%, for one common lithium-ion type. So charge after each day's use, and avoid running the battery right down.

Researchers at the University of Michigan also advise not leaving lithium-ion batteries at 0% or 100% for long periods.

Keep It Cool

Heat is the biggest enemy of lithium-ion batteries. Battery University treats anything above 30°C as high temperature, and heat together with a full charge does the most harm. In Indian summers, that means:

  • Do not leave the chair or battery in a parked car or in direct sun.
  • Do not charge in a hot room or next to a heater or stove.
  • Avoid charging in very cold conditions too.
  • Keep the battery dry and protect it from knocks.

Storing a Chair You Are Not Using

Do not store the battery fully charged or flat. In Battery University tests, a lithium-ion battery kept for a year at 25°C kept about 96% of its capacity at 40% charge, but only about 80% at full charge. We suggest storing it part-charged in a cool, dry place and topping it up every few weeks, or as the maker advises.

Flying With a Lithium Battery

Airlines have their own rules for lithium-ion batteries on mobility aids. Check with the airline before you book, and carry the battery details from your chair's manual.

Signs the Battery Needs Replacing

  • The chair goes noticeably less far on a full charge.
  • The charge gauge drops quickly or jumps around.
  • The battery case is swollen, cracked, leaking or gets very hot. Stop using it at once and contact your supplier.

Electric Wheelchair Battery Care: Key Facts

  • Lithium-ion wheelchair batteries have no memory, so partial top-ups are fine.
  • Shallow discharges give far more cycles than full drains.
  • Heat above 30°C speeds up battery ageing, especially when fully charged.
  • Store a battery part-charged in a cool, dry place.
  • Use only the supplied charger and charge indoors on a hard surface.

Electric Wheelchair Battery Care: FAQs

Should I charge my electric wheelchair every day?

Charge after each day's use and avoid running the battery flat. Lithium-ion batteries have no memory, so partial top-ups are fine.

Can I leave my wheelchair charging overnight?

Follow your chair's manual. Use only the supplied charger, charge indoors on a hard surface with good airflow, and do not cover the charger.

How do I store an electric wheelchair battery?

Part-charged, in a cool, dry place, and topped up every few weeks or as the maker advises. Do not store it flat or fully charged in heat.

Does heat damage wheelchair batteries?

Yes. Temperatures above 30 degrees C speed up lithium-ion ageing, especially when the battery is fully charged. Do not leave it in a hot car or in direct sun.

When should I replace my wheelchair battery?

When range drops noticeably, the charge gauge falls quickly, or the battery is swollen, leaking or very hot. Stop using a damaged battery at once.
