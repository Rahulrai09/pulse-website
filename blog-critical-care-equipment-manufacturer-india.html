What Counts as Critical Care Equipment

Critical care equipment covers the machines that keep the sickest patients stable and monitored — typically in ICUs, operation theatres, and emergency departments. This includes:

  • ICU and transport ventilators
  • Anaesthesia workstations
  • Patient monitors (5-para, 7-para, and higher)
  • ECG machines (3, 6, and 12-channel)
  • Infusion and syringe pumps

Most of these fall into CDSCO's higher-risk device classes (Class B and above), which means the manufacturing and regulatory bar is meaningfully stricter than for low-risk consumables.

Why Manufacturing Depth Matters More Here Than in Other Categories

Critical care equipment failure has immediate clinical consequences. That is why service response time and spare-parts availability matter as much as the initial purchase price. A ventilator with a great spec sheet but a three-week wait for a replacement part isn't a good purchase for an ICU. This is where evaluating the manufacturer's service network — not just the product — becomes essential.

What to Verify Before Buying

  • CDSCO manufacturing licence and device classification (Class B, C, or D for most critical care equipment)
  • ISO 13485 certificate and its exact facility/product scope
  • Clinical evaluation or performance evidence for the specific model
  • Documented AMC/CMC terms and what they actually cover
  • Spare-parts availability and average repair turnaround time in India
  • Installation, commissioning, and clinician training support
For critical care equipment specifically, the manufacturer's service commitment matters as much as the product spec sheet — ask what happens when something breaks, not just what it does when it works.

Critical Care Equipment Manufacturer in India: Key Facts

  • Under India's Medical Devices Rules, 2017, devices fall into four risk classes: A (low), B (low-moderate), C (moderate-high) and D (high).
  • Most critical care equipment, such as ventilators and patient monitors, falls in Class B or higher.
  • State Licensing Authorities license Class A and B manufacturing; the Central Licensing Authority regulates Class C and D.
  • ISO 13485:2016 is the international quality management standard for medical device makers.
  • Pulse's critical care range covers ICU ventilators, anaesthesia workstations, ECG machines, 5-para and 7-para monitors, and infusion and syringe pumps.

Critical Care Equipment Manufacturer in India: FAQs

Who are the main critical care equipment manufacturers in India?

India's critical care equipment manufacturers include Skanray Technologies, BPL Medical Technologies, Trivitron Healthcare, and horizontal OEMs like Pulse, covering ventilators, patient monitors, anaesthesia machines, and ECG equipment. Buyers should verify each manufacturer's CDSCO licence and product-specific certification before purchase.

What critical care equipment is manufactured in India?

Indian manufacturers produce ICU ventilators, anaesthesia workstations, patient monitors, ECG machines, infusion pumps, and syringe pumps, among other critical care equipment, with varying levels of import dependence for components across manufacturers.

What should a hospital check before buying critical care equipment from an Indian manufacturer?

Check the device's CDSCO risk classification, the manufacturer's current licence, ISO 13485 certification scope, clinical evidence, installation and commissioning support, AMC/CMC terms, and spare-parts availability before finalising a purchase.
