What Makes One Machine Cost More Than Another

1. How well it controls fluid removal

During dialysis, the machine takes extra water out of the body. This is called ultrafiltration. Good machines remove water very exactly, so the patient does not lose too much or too little. Better control costs more, but it keeps patients safer.

2. Safety alarms

A good machine checks for air bubbles, blood leaks and wrong pressure all the time. It stops the blood pump and sounds an alarm if something goes wrong. More checks mean a higher price.

3. Treatment tracking

Some machines measure how well each session cleaned the blood. Doctors call this Kt/V. It helps them change the treatment when needed.

4. Cleaning (disinfection)

The machine must be cleaned inside after every patient. Machines that clean themselves save staff time and lower the risk of infection.

5. Battery backup

Power cuts happen. A machine with battery backup keeps the blood moving safely until power comes back.

6. Service and spare parts

A machine that sits broken means patients miss their dialysis. Ask how fast a service engineer can reach you and where spare parts are kept. Read more in our guide on why after-sales service matters.

Costs After You Buy the Machine

The machine is only the start. Plan for these costs too:

  • Water plant (RO plant): dialysis needs very clean water. Every centre needs a water cleaning plant.
  • Dialyser (the filter): this is where the blood gets cleaned. See Pulse dialysers.
  • Blood tubing: carries blood from the patient to the machine and back. See PD-Linepro blood tubing sets.
  • Needles for the fistula: used to connect the patient at each session. See PD-Canova AV fistula needles.
  • Pressure filters: small filters that keep the machine's pressure sensors clean. See PD-TP transducer protectors.
  • Catheters: for patients who do not yet have a fistula. See PD-Cath haemodialysis catheters.
  • Cleaning chemicals, service contract (AMC) and trained staff.

A machine that costs a little more but breaks down less often can cost less over five years than the cheapest one.

New or Refurbished: Which Is Better?

A refurbished machine costs less on day one. But it may be old, spare parts may be hard to find, and the warranty is often short. A new machine costs more, but comes with a full warranty and the latest safety features. If you buy refurbished, ask for the machine's age, its service record and who will repair it.

Before you buy: a quick checklist

  1. Count how many patients you treat each day and how many machines you need.
  2. Check that you have, or can set up, a clean water (RO) plant.
  3. Ask if the price includes GST, delivery, installation and staff training.
  4. Ask how long the warranty is and what the service contract costs after that.
  5. Ask the cost of each session's consumables, not just the machine.
  6. Ask how fast an engineer can reach you if the machine stops.

A Note for Patients and Families

Most people in India get haemodialysis at a hospital or dialysis centre, not at home. Under the government's Pradhan Mantri National Dialysis Programme, started in 2016, district hospitals offer free dialysis to patients below the poverty line. Ask your nearest district hospital about it. Some patients can also do peritoneal dialysis at home. Learn more in our guide on peritoneal dialysis vs haemodialysis.

Dialysis Machine Price in India: FAQs

How much does a dialysis machine cost in India?

A new haemodialysis machine usually costs about Rs 5 lakh to Rs 15 lakh. High-end imported machines can cost Rs 15 lakh to Rs 25 lakh or more. A refurbished machine costs about Rs 2 lakh to Rs 5 lakh.

Is a refurbished dialysis machine a good idea?

It costs less to buy, but check the machine's age, its service record, the warranty and who will repair it. Old machines can be hard to fix if spare parts are no longer made.

What else do I need besides the machine?

You need a clean water (RO) plant, dialysers, blood tubing, fistula needles, transducer protectors, cleaning chemicals, a service contract and trained staff.

Is dialysis free in government hospitals in India?

Under the Pradhan Mantri National Dialysis Programme, started in 2016, district hospitals offer free dialysis to patients below the poverty line.

What does one dialysis session cost in India?

NephroPlus (October 2025) puts haemodialysis at about Rs 1,000 to Rs 4,000 per session: around Rs 500 to Rs 1,500 at government-aided centres and Rs 1,800 to Rs 3,500 at private centres. At two to three sessions a week, that is roughly Rs 24,000 to Rs 50,000 a month.

How much does a home dialysis machine cost in India?

Most home dialysis in India is peritoneal dialysis, which mainly costs a monthly supply of fluid bags rather than a large machine; some patients use an automated cycler at night. Home haemodialysis machines are rare in India, so ask your nephrologist and supplier for an itemised quote that includes water treatment, training and service.
