Circumcision Stapler vs Traditional Surgery: 2024 Study Results

Marketing material around disposable circumcision devices tends to oversimplify — it's framed as "fast, bloodless, better." The clinical evidence is more balanced than that. A recent peer-reviewed comparative study measured both methods on the same clinical ward, and the picture is more useful than either side's brochure suggests.

PBy Pulse Team · Updated October 15, 2026 · 7 min read
Share

Short answer: The 2024 study (301 patients, Frontiers in Pediatrics) found the stapler cuts operative time by roughly half (9.87 vs 21.09 minutes) and reduces blood loss (3.92 vs 9.85 mL). But traditional circumcision wins on wound healing time (8.36 vs 14.15 days), less severe edge swelling, and lower rebleeding. Infection, dehiscence, and aesthetic satisfaction were statistically comparable. Each method has honest trade-offs.

The Study, in Brief

Published in Frontiers in Pediatrics in July 2024, the study (Zhang et al.) is a retrospective comparison from a single paediatric surgery department covering January 2022 through December 2023. The researchers compared 155 children treated with a disposable circumcision stapler against 146 children treated with traditional circumcision, all under local anaesthesia. It's not a randomised controlled trial, so the usual caveats about selection bias apply — but a 300-patient head-to-head on the same ward is more than most equipment-level comparisons offer.

What the Stapler Is Genuinely Better At

  • Operative time. Roughly half the duration. For a facility running volume lists under local anaesthesia, cutting ~11 minutes off every case compounds significantly across a day's schedule — and OT time is often the most expensive single input in the cost equation, which the per-case figure above doesn't capture.
  • Blood loss. Less than half the volume. Real benefit for very young paediatric patients or any patient where minimising intraoperative bleeding matters clinically.
  • Standardisation of result. The stapler reduces operator-skill variability — the finished result is more consistent across different surgeons, which is a real advantage in training settings or facilities with mixed-experience surgical teams.
  • Rebleeding risk. Lower (1.29% vs 6.16%). Partly a function of how the stapler simultaneously resects and seals the wound edge.

What Traditional Circumcision Is Still Better At

  • Healing time. Noticeably faster (8.36 vs 14.15 days). This is the most consistent trade-off the stapler carries, and it should be part of patient counselling — fast surgery does not mean fast recovery.
  • Severe edge swelling. Less common (10.27% vs 25.16%). The compression ring the stapler leaves behind creates a specific inflammatory pattern that traditional suturing avoids.
  • Operator feel. For complex or atypical anatomies, traditional circumcision gives the surgeon direct tactile control that the stapler mechanism doesn't allow. Experienced surgeons sometimes prefer traditional for difficult cases precisely for this reason.

Where the Two Methods Tie

Three outcomes most patients and parents actually care about showed no significant difference between methods: infection rates, wound dehiscence, and aesthetic satisfaction scores. Pain also did not differ meaningfully. In other words — the dramatic "stapler is safer" framing some vendor material uses isn't supported by this evidence; the two methods are safer in different, specific ways.

Neither method is universally better. The stapler trades slower healing and more edge swelling for faster surgery and less bleeding. Traditional circumcision trades the reverse. Pick the one that fits the case, not the one that sounds more modern.

How a Hospital Should Think About Procurement

A Disposable Stapler Earns Its Place If…

  • Your facility runs high circumcision volume (a shorter per-case time saves meaningful OT hours over a month).
  • Your surgical team includes trainees or mixed-experience operators where result standardisation matters.
  • You're setting up a mobile or camp-based circumcision service where speed, bloodlessness, and reduced sterilisation burden all compound.
  • Younger paediatric patients where even small blood loss volumes matter clinically.

Traditional Circumcision Remains the Right Call If…

  • Patient anatomy is atypical, which may complicate the stapler's fixed geometry.
  • Faster post-op recovery is clinically or socially important for the individual case.
  • Your surgeon is highly experienced in open technique and your case volume doesn't justify stapler stock.
  • Supply chain for disposable staplers is unreliable at your location — running out mid-list is worse than always using the older method well.

Realistic Model for Most Facilities: Stock Both

The most honest reading of the evidence is that both techniques belong on a circumcision service's shelf. The stapler as the default for routine, high-volume paediatric cases; traditional circumcision available for atypical anatomy, revision cases, and surgeon preference. Treating this as an either/or technology-choice decision oversimplifies what the clinical data actually shows.

Circumcision Stapler vs Traditional Surgery: FAQs

Is a disposable circumcision stapler always better than traditional circumcision?

No. The 2024 comparative study of 301 patients found that the stapler reduced operative time and blood loss significantly, but traditional circumcision had faster wound healing, less severe edge swelling, and less rebleeding. Infection and aesthetic satisfaction were comparable. Each method has measurable trade-offs — the right choice depends on patient context, operator preference, and facility workflow.

How much faster is a stapler procedure than traditional circumcision?

In the 2024 study, mean operative time was 9.87 minutes with the stapler versus 21.09 minutes with traditional circumcision — roughly half as long. For a facility running high patient volumes under local anaesthesia, that time saving compounds significantly across a day's list.

Does stapler circumcision heal faster than traditional surgery?

The opposite — the 2024 study found traditional circumcision had a mean healing time of 8.36 days versus 14.15 days for the stapler. This is one of the most consistent trade-offs the stapler carries, and should factor into patient counselling. Faster surgery does not mean faster recovery.

What about cost — is a disposable stapler cheaper than traditional circumcision?

Marginally more expensive per case in the 2024 study (approximately USD 264 vs USD 249), though the gap is small enough that total procedure cost often depends more on local pricing, OT time rate, and the volume discount a facility negotiates with the stapler supplier.
