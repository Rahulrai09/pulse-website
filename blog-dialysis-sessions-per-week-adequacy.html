How Often Is Dialysis Needed? Sessions per Week, Kt/V and URR

Most people on haemodialysis go three times a week. This guide explains how long sessions last, how the dialysis team checks that treatment is enough (Kt/V and URR), the usual targets, and what helps when the numbers are low.

Most people on in-centre haemodialysis go 3 days a week, and each session usually takes 3 to 5 hours, according to the US National Kidney Foundation. Teams check that dialysis is enough with two tests: a URR of at least 65% and a Kt/V of at least 1.2, usually measured once a month.

How Often and How Long

TypeHow oftenSession length
In-centre haemodialysisUsually 3 days a weekAbout 3 to 5 hours
Home haemodialysis (short)4 to 6 days a weekAbout 2 to 3 hours
Home haemodialysis (overnight)Some nights each weekLonger sessions while sleeping

These patterns are from the US National Kidney Foundation (2026). Your kidney doctor prescribes the exact time. Home dialysis needs about 6 to 10 weeks of training.

What Is URR?

URR (urea reduction ratio) is the percentage of urea, a waste product, removed in one session. It is worked out from blood tests taken before and after dialysis. For people on dialysis three times a week, teams usually look for a URR of at least 65%.

What Is Kt/V?

Kt/V compares how much blood the dialyser cleans of urea in a session (K, clearance, times t, time) with the water in the body (V). It is the main measure of dialysis dose.

MeasureUsual target (3 times a week)
URRAt least 65%
Kt/V (minimum delivered)At least 1.2
Kt/V (target per session)1.4

The 1.4 target and 1.2 minimum come from the US KDOQI guideline (2015). A Kt/V of 1.2 is roughly the same as a URR of 63%. Tests are usually done about once a month, every 12 to 14 sessions.

Does More Dialysis Always Help?

Not by these numbers alone. A large US study (HEMO) found that pushing Kt/V higher than the usual targets did not improve health or survival. Teams also look at fluid removal, blood pressure, lab results, symptoms and remaining urine output.

What Helps When the Numbers Are Low

  • Longer sessions: going from 3 to 4 hours raises the dose by about a third.
  • Better blood flow: adults should generally reach 350 ml a minute or more through the dialyser.
  • Check the access: problems with the fistula or catheter, or reversed needles, can cut the dose.
  • Do not skip or shorten sessions.

Common Side Effects During Dialysis

Low blood pressure, cramps, headache, nausea, dizziness and tiredness after treatment are common. Removing fluid too fast can cause many of these, which is one reason to limit fluid between sessions as your team advises. See our low blood pressure and cramps guide.

Dialysis Sessions and Adequacy: Key Facts

  • Most people on in-centre haemodialysis go 3 days a week for about 3 to 5 hours each.
  • Usual targets: URR of at least 65% and Kt/V of at least 1.2.
  • KDOQI (2015) recommends a target Kt/V of 1.4 per session for thrice-weekly dialysis.
  • Kt/V and URR are usually tested once a month.
  • The HEMO study found a Kt/V above the usual targets did not improve survival.

Dialysis Sessions and Adequacy: FAQs

How many times a week is dialysis needed?

Most people on in-centre haemodialysis go 3 days a week. Home dialysis can be 4 to 6 shorter sessions a week. Your kidney doctor sets the schedule.

How long does a dialysis session take?

In-centre sessions usually take about 3 to 5 hours. Home sessions can be about 2 to 3 hours, done more often.

What is a good Kt/V for dialysis?

At least 1.2 delivered, with a target of 1.4 per session for dialysis three times a week (KDOQI 2015).

What is URR in dialysis?

The urea reduction ratio, the percentage of urea removed in a session. Teams usually look for at least 65%.

How can I improve my dialysis adequacy?

Do not skip or shorten sessions. Your team may lengthen sessions, raise blood flow or check your fistula or catheter.
