ECG Lead Placement: 12-Lead (V1 to V6), 5-Lead and 3-Lead

A 12-lead ECG uses 10 electrodes to give 12 views of the heart. Putting them in the right place matters, because a small shift can change the tracing. This guide shows where each electrode goes, the colour codes, how to find the landmarks and how to avoid common mistakes.

A 12-lead ECG uses 10 electrodes: 4 on the limbs and 6 on the chest. V1 and V2 go in the 4th intercostal space either side of the breastbone, V4 in the 5th space at the mid-collarbone line, V3 halfway between V2 and V4, and V5 and V6 level with V4 towards the armpit.

Chest Electrode Positions (V1 to V6)

ElectrodePosition
V14th intercostal space, right edge of the breastbone
V24th intercostal space, left edge of the breastbone
V3Halfway between V2 and V4
V45th intercostal space, midclavicular line (straight down from the middle of the collarbone)
V5Left anterior axillary line (front of the armpit), level with V4
V6Left midaxillary line (middle of the armpit), level with V4 and V5

Place V4 before V3, so V3 can sit exactly halfway between V2 and V4.

Limb Electrode Positions

ElectrodePosition
RARight arm, inner wrist or forearm
LALeft arm, inner wrist or forearm
RLRight leg, inner ankle or lower leg
LLLeft leg, inner ankle or lower leg

Limb electrodes can sit higher on the limbs if needed, as long as both sides match.

5-Lead and 3-Lead ECG Placement (Monitors)

Bedside monitors and transport monitors usually use 3 or 5 electrodes instead of 10. The limb electrodes move onto the chest, which cuts down movement noise. These are for watching the heart rhythm over time, not for a full diagnostic ECG.

ElectrodeWhere it goesAHA colourIEC colour
RAJust below the right collarbone, near the shoulderWhiteRed
LAJust below the left collarbone, near the shoulderBlackYellow
LLLower left side of the belly or lower left rib cageRedGreen
RL (5-lead only)Lower right side of the belly or lower right rib cageGreenBlack
V (5-lead only)One chest position, usually V1BrownWhite
  • 3-lead: RA, LA and LL. The monitor can show lead I, II or III.
  • 5-lead: adds RL and one chest electrode (V). Five electrodes give seven tracings: I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF and one V lead.
  • Memory aid (AHA colours): "smoke over fire" (black above red on the left) and "snow over grass" (white above green on the right).
  • Best rhythm lead: lead II is the lead most often used for continuous monitoring.

If the monitor shows something worrying, such as a new rhythm problem, we suggest taking a full 12-lead ECG. Our patient monitor normal values guide explains what the other numbers on the screen mean.

Posterior Leads (V7 to V9)

A standard 12-lead ECG does not look directly at the back wall of the heart. When doctors suspect a posterior heart attack, they add three electrodes on the left side of the back, all at the same height as V6:

  • V7: left posterior axillary line (the back edge of the armpit).
  • V8: tip of the left shoulder blade.
  • V9: between V8 and the spine, beside the spinal muscles.

The chest cables V4, V5 and V6 are usually moved to these points. Mark the printout as "posterior" so it is not read as a normal 12-lead ECG.

What Each ECG Lead Looks At

Each lead looks at the heart from a different angle. Groups of leads look at the same wall of the heart:

Heart wallLeads
Inferior (bottom)II, III, aVF
Lateral (left side)I, aVL, V5, V6
Anterior (front)V1 to V4

This is why correct placement matters: an electrode in the wrong space can make one wall look abnormal when it is not. See ECG normal values for the normal PR, QRS and QT ranges.

How to Find the 4th Intercostal Space

  1. Put two fingers at the top of the breastbone and slide down until you feel a ridge (the angle of Louis).
  2. Slide to the right into the groove between two ribs. This is the 2nd intercostal space.
  3. Move down over the next two ribs to reach the 4th space. Place V1 here, and V2 in the same space on the left.

Preparing the Patient

  • Explain the test, give privacy and offer a chaperone.
  • Have the patient lie back at about 45 degrees, relaxed, with arms and legs uncrossed.
  • Clean oily or sweaty skin, dry it, and clip hair at electrode sites.
  • In women, place V4 to V6 under the breast, along the 5th intercostal space line.
  • Ask the patient to stay still, breathe normally and not talk during the recording.

Common Problems

  • Wrong position: even small shifts can change the tracing and lead to a wrong diagnosis.
  • Wavy baseline or missing lead: usually poor electrode contact. Re-prepare the skin and replace the electrode.
  • Changed positions: if you must move an electrode, note it on the printout.

12-Lead ECG Electrode Placement: Key Facts

  • A 12-lead ECG uses 10 electrodes: 4 limb and 6 chest.
  • V1 and V2 go in the 4th intercostal space either side of the breastbone.
  • V4 goes in the 5th intercostal space at the midclavicular line; V3 halfway between V2 and V4.
  • V5 and V6 sit level with V4 at the front and middle of the armpit.
  • AHA and IEC use different colour codes, so check the cable labels.
  • Monitors use 3 electrodes (RA, LA, LL) or 5 (plus RL and one chest lead); lead II is the usual rhythm lead.
  • Posterior leads V7 to V9 sit on the left back at the level of V6.

12-Lead ECG Electrode Placement: FAQs

Where do you place V1 to V6 on an ECG?

V1 and V2 in the 4th intercostal space at the right and left edges of the breastbone, V4 in the 5th space at the midclavicular line, V3 between V2 and V4, and V5 and V6 level with V4 towards the armpit.

How many electrodes are used in a 12-lead ECG?

Ten: four on the limbs and six on the chest. Together they give 12 views (leads) of the heart.

Where do the limb leads go?

RA and LA on the inner right and left wrists or forearms, RL and LL on the inner right and left ankles or lower legs, placed evenly on both sides.

What are the ECG lead colours?

In the AHA system RA is white, LA black, RL green and LL red. The IEC system uses red, yellow, black and green. Check your cable labels.

Why does an ECG show a wavy baseline?

Usually poor electrode contact. Clean and dry the skin, clip hair and replace the electrode.

How is an ECG done on a woman?

The same 12-lead positions are used. A woman is usually asked to remove her top and bra so the chest electrodes stick to the skin, and is offered a cover once the leads are on. She can also ask for a chaperone to be in the room.

What is the 5-lead ECG placement?

RA and LA go just below the right and left collarbones, RL and LL on the lower right and lower left belly or rib cage, and the brown chest electrode (V) usually in the V1 position. Five electrodes show seven tracings: I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF and one V lead.

Which lead is best for heart rhythm monitoring?

Lead II is the lead most often used for continuous monitoring, because it usually shows the P wave and QRS clearly.

Where are the posterior ECG leads placed?

All at the level of V6 on the left back: V7 at the posterior axillary line, V8 at the tip of the shoulder blade, and V9 between V8 and the spine.
