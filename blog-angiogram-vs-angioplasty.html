Angiogram vs Angioplasty: What Is the Difference?

An angiogram and an angioplasty use the same thin tube and the same X-ray room, so people often mix them up. This guide explains what each one does, how long it takes, what recovery looks like, and where a stent fits in.

PBy Pulse Team · Updated November 26, 2026 · 4 min read
Share

An angiogram is a test: dye and X-rays show whether the heart arteries are narrowed or blocked. An angioplasty is a treatment: a small balloon opens the narrowed artery, and usually a stent (a small metal mesh tube) is left in to keep it open. Both are done through a thin tube in the wrist or groin, often in the same session.

A Test vs a Treatment

A coronary angiogram (also called coronary angiography) is a test. A thin, flexible tube called a catheter goes into an artery, usually near the groin or wrist. A special dye is injected through it, and X-rays show the heart arteries (NHS). It is done to find out what is wrong, not to treat it (Manchester University NHS Foundation Trust).

A coronary angioplasty is a treatment. A catheter with a small balloon at its tip is guided to the narrowed part of the artery. The balloon is blown up to widen the artery, and a stent is usually placed to keep it open (British Heart Foundation). Doctors also call it PCI (percutaneous coronary intervention) or PTCA.

Can Both Be Done in the Same Session?

Yes. The NHS says treatment such as inserting a balloon to open a narrowed artery is sometimes done at the same time as the angiogram (NHS). The British Heart Foundation describes the angiogram as one part of the angioplasty procedure: dye is injected so the arteries show on X-ray before the balloon goes in (BHF).

So the usual order is: angiogram first, to find the blockage, then angioplasty straight away if the doctor and patient agree it is the right treatment. Some patients instead go home after the angiogram and come back later, or are advised medicines or bypass surgery (Heart Foundation NZ).

Angioplasty vs Stent: Are They Different?

A stent is not a separate operation. It is a small metal mesh tube put in during an angioplasty. The balloon opens the artery, and the stent stays behind as a scaffold to hold it open (BHF).

  • Drug-eluting stents are coated with a medicine that lowers the chance of the artery narrowing again.
  • Bare metal stents have no coating, and the artery is more likely to narrow again (NHS).
  • Balloon only: sometimes no stent is left. A drug-coated balloon can deliver medicine to the artery wall instead. See our drug-coated balloon vs stent guide.

The NHS says blood-thinning treatment after a drug-eluting stent is needed for up to 1 year (NHS). Your cardiologist decides the exact medicines and how long to take them.

Recovery: How Is It Different?

After an angiogram, most people go home the same day. Bruising and soreness where the tube went in are common for a few days or weeks (NHS).

After an angioplasty, the British Heart Foundation says you may leave about 2 hours after a wrist approach, or about 5 to 6 hours after a groin approach. Avoid driving for at least a week. After a planned, uncomplicated angioplasty, some people are back at work within a few days, depending on the job (BHF). For day-by-day advice, read our recovery after angioplasty guide.

What About Cost?

An angioplasty costs much more than an angiogram, because it adds a balloon, one or more stents and blood-thinning medicines. For sourced Indian prices, see our guides to coronary angiography cost, angioplasty cost and the heart stent price cap.

Angiogram vs Angioplasty: Key Facts

  • An angiogram is a test; an angioplasty is a treatment.
  • Both use a catheter through the wrist or groin, usually under local anaesthetic.
  • Each usually takes 30 minutes to 2 hours (NHS, BHF).
  • A stent is placed during an angioplasty; it is not a separate operation.
  • Blood-thinning medicines are often needed for up to 1 year after a drug-eluting stent.

Angiogram vs Angioplasty: FAQs

Is an angiogram the same as an angioplasty?

No. An angiogram is a test that uses dye and X-rays to show narrowed or blocked heart arteries. An angioplasty is a treatment that opens the narrowed artery with a balloon, usually leaving a stent in place.

Can angioplasty be done during an angiogram?

Yes. If the angiogram shows a narrowing that needs treatment, angioplasty is often done straight away in the same session, using the same catheter route.

Is angioplasty the same as a stent?

Not quite. Angioplasty is the procedure that opens the artery with a balloon. A stent is a small metal mesh tube that is usually put in during the angioplasty to keep the artery open.

How long do an angiogram and an angioplasty take?

Both usually take between 30 minutes and 2 hours, according to the NHS and the British Heart Foundation. It can sometimes take longer.

Is an angiogram painful?

It should not be. You are usually awake, and a local anaesthetic numbs the skin where the tube goes in. You may feel some pushing and pulling, and bruising afterwards is common.

When can I go home after an angioplasty?

Most people go home the same day or the next day. The British Heart Foundation says it can be about 2 hours after a wrist approach and about 5 to 6 hours after a groin approach.
