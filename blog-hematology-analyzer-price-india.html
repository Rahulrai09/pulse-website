3-Part vs 5-Part: What Is the Difference?

Both count red cells, white cells and platelets and report the standard CBC. The difference is in the white cell differential:

  • 3-part: lymphocytes, mid-size cells and granulocytes.
  • 5-part: neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils and basophils.

What Drives the Price

1. Differential and technology

5-part analyzers use extra optical or chemical methods to separate all five white cell types, which raises cost.

2. Throughput

Analyzers running 60 to 72 tests an hour suit most small and mid-sized labs. Higher-throughput systems with autoloaders cost more.

3. Reagent system

Fewer reagents mean lower running cost and simpler stock management. Closed (RFID-tagged) reagent systems improve traceability.

4. Software, QC and connectivity

Built-in QC programs, large result storage, and LIS connectivity matter for accredited labs.

5. Service and reagent supply

An analyzer is only useful if reagents arrive on time and an engineer can fix faults quickly.

Hematology Analyzer Price in India: FAQs

How much does a hematology analyzer cost in India?

Online listings in October 2026 show fully automatic 3-part hematology analyzers at about Rs 1.7 lakh to Rs 4 lakh, and entry 5-part analyzers at about Rs 3.6 lakh to Rs 3.9 lakh. High-throughput 5-part systems for large labs cost considerably more.

What is the difference between a 3-part and 5-part hematology analyzer?

A 3-part analyzer splits white blood cells into three groups: lymphocytes, mid cells and granulocytes. A 5-part analyzer separates all five types: neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils and basophils, which helps with infections, allergies and blood disorders.

Is a 3-part hematology analyzer enough for a small lab?

For most clinics, nursing homes and small labs running routine CBCs, a 3-part analyzer is usually enough. Labs serving hospitals with oncology, paediatrics or many abnormal samples often prefer 5-part.

What are the running costs of a hematology analyzer?

Reagents (diluent, lyse and cleaners), controls and calibrators, printer paper, and an AMC. The cost per test depends mostly on reagent use, so analyzers that need fewer reagents usually cost less to run.

What should I check before buying a hematology analyzer?

Check 3-part or 5-part, throughput (tests per hour), sample volume, number of reagents and whether they are closed or open, QC features, data storage and LIS connectivity, cyanide-free haemoglobin, and local service and reagent supply.
