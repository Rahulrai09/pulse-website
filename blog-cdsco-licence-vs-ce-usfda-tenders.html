CDSCO Licence vs CE and US FDA: What Hospital Tenders Must Ask For

Many hospital tenders still ask for a CE mark or US FDA approval. In November 2025, India's device regulator told buyers what must come first. This guide explains the rule and how to write a fair tender.

Under a CDSCO circular of 17 November 2025, hospitals and other buyers in India must make an Indian licence, from CDSCO or the State Licensing Authority under the Medical Devices Rules 2017, a compulsory tender condition. A CE mark or US FDA approval cannot replace it. Buyers may still ask for them, but only as extras.

What the CDSCO Circular Says

CDSCO (the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation) is India's medical device regulator. On 17 November 2025 it sent a circular (file MED-13/87/2025) to all buyers, including hospitals and health institutions.

  • The problem it found: many hospitals asked for US FDA or CE certificates as a must-have in the technical bid.
  • The rule: under the Medical Devices Rules 2017, a device can be imported, made, sold or supplied in India only with a valid Indian licence.
  • What buyers must do: make the CDSCO or state licence a compulsory condition in every tender.
  • Foreign approvals: CE or US FDA can be asked for only on top of the Indian licence, never instead of it.

Which Licence a Device Needs

Indian devices fall into four risk classes: A (low risk), B, C and D (high risk). The class decides who gives the licence.

LicenceWho issues it
Import licence (all classes)CDSCO
Manufacturing licence, Class C and DCDSCO
Manufacturing licence, Class A and BState or UT Licensing Authority
Sale and distribution licence (all classes)State or UT Licensing Authority

CDSCO gives its licences only after a technical review and a check of the maker's quality system. For more on risk classes, see our complete guide to buying medical equipment.

Can a Tender Still Ask for CE or US FDA?

Yes, but only as an extra. The circular lets buyers add other certificates "over and above" the Indian licence. Indian makers welcome the rule but say this last line can still be used to shut them out. The device makers' body AiMeD has asked CDSCO to remove it.

An older rule points the same way. In October 2018, the Department of Pharmaceuticals told public buyers to follow Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) norms, and said US FDA or CE should not be made compulsory where a BIS standard exists.

BIS Standards for Medical Devices

Under Rule 7 of the Medical Devices Rules, a device must meet BIS standards, or standards the Health Ministry notifies. In October 2025, CDSCO published a list of about 1,700 BIS standards for medical devices and hospital planning, in about 20 groups. Large groups include dentistry (235), hospital and disposable products (181), surgical instruments (123) and electrical medical, imaging and radiotherapy equipment (107). There are also 61 standards for hospital planning.

A tender can name the BIS standard a device must meet. This is a clear, fair test that both Indian and foreign makers can pass.

How to Write a Fair Tender

  1. Make the Indian licence compulsory. Ask for a copy of the CDSCO or state licence that covers the exact model you are buying.
  2. Check the licence. Match the maker's name, the device name and the risk class to the licence, and check that it is still valid.
  3. Name the standards. List the BIS or IEC standards that matter for the device, such as electrical safety.
  4. Ask for test reports from an accredited lab, and the maker's quality certificate (such as ISO 13485).
  5. Treat CE or US FDA as optional. If you want them, give them a few extra marks rather than making them a pass-or-fail rule.
  6. Judge on service too: warranty, spare parts, response time and training. Our after-sales service guide explains what to ask.

CDSCO Licence vs CE and US FDA: Key Facts

  • A CDSCO circular of 17 November 2025 says buyers must make an Indian CDSCO or state licence a compulsory tender condition.
  • A CE mark or US FDA approval cannot replace the Indian licence; buyers may ask for them only as extras.
  • CDSCO issues import licences and Class C and D manufacturing licences; states issue Class A and B manufacturing and all sale licences.
  • Devices must meet BIS standards under Rule 7 of the Medical Devices Rules; CDSCO listed about 1,700 of them in October 2025.
  • Since 2018, public buyers have been told not to make US FDA or CE compulsory where a BIS standard exists.

CDSCO Licence vs CE and US FDA: FAQs

Is a CDSCO licence mandatory for hospital procurement of medical devices?

Yes. A CDSCO circular of 17 November 2025 asks all buyers, including hospitals, to make a CDSCO or State Licensing Authority licence under the Medical Devices Rules 2017 a compulsory condition when buying medical devices.

Can a hospital tender ask for CE or US FDA approval?

Only as an extra. The CDSCO circular says such certificates may be asked for over and above the Indian licence, but they cannot replace it.

Who issues licences for medical devices in India?

CDSCO issues import licences for all classes and manufacturing licences for Class C and D devices. State Licensing Authorities issue manufacturing licences for Class A and B devices and sale licences for all classes.

Do medical devices in India have to meet BIS standards?

Yes. Under Rule 7 of the Medical Devices Rules, devices must meet BIS standards or standards notified by the Health Ministry. CDSCO listed about 1,700 relevant BIS standards in October 2025.

Is a CE mark enough to sell a medical device in India?

No. A CE mark is a European approval. To import, make or sell a device in India, it needs a licence from CDSCO or the State Licensing Authority.
