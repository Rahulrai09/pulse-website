Fowler's Position Explained: Semi, High and Trendelenburg

Nurses raise or tilt a hospital bed to help patients breathe, eat, rest and recover. This guide explains the Fowler's positions and their angles, the Trendelenburg tilts used in ICUs and theatres, and what to look for in a bed that sets them easily.

PBy Pulse Team · Updated November 23, 2026 · 4 min read
Share

In Fowler's position the head of the bed is raised to 45 to 60 degrees. Semi-Fowler's is a lower 30 to 45 degrees, and high Fowler's is a near-upright 60 to 90 degrees. Raising the head eases breathing, and sitting upright lowers aspiration risk when a feeding tube is placed.

Why Raising the Head Helps

Sitting up lets the chest expand more and takes pressure off the lungs, so breathing is easier. That is why Fowler's position is the most common resting position for patients in hospital wards and emergency rooms. High Fowler's position, with the patient nearly upright, lowers the risk of fluid going into the lungs when a tube is passed through the nose or mouth into the stomach.

Trendelenburg and Reverse Trendelenburg

In Trendelenburg the whole bed tilts head-down. It increases blood flow back to the heart and gives surgeons a better view in some abdominal operations. But it pushes the abdomen up against the lungs and raises pressure in the head and eyes, so it is used for limited times and with care. Reverse Trendelenburg tilts the bed head-up for upper abdominal surgery, but can lower blood pressure.

Positioning Safely

  • Raise the knee-rest when the back is raised, so the patient does not slide down the bed.
  • Check pressure points often. Sitting up puts more weight on the tailbone and heels. See our bedsore prevention guide.
  • Use side rails safely and keep the bed low when the patient is unattended.

These tips are our suggestions for bedside care; follow your hospital's nursing policy.

Fowler's Position: Key Facts

  • Fowler's position raises the head of the bed to 45 to 60 degrees.
  • Semi-Fowler's is 30 to 45 degrees; high Fowler's is 60 to 90 degrees.
  • Raising the head eases breathing and lowers aspiration risk during tube placement.
  • Trendelenburg tilts the bed head-down; reverse Trendelenburg tilts it head-up.
  • Raise the knee-rest with the back-rest so the patient does not slide down.

Fowler's Position: FAQs

What is Fowler's position?

The patient lies on their back with the head of the bed raised to 45 to 60 degrees. It is the most common resting position in hospital.

What angle is semi-Fowler's position?

30 to 45 degrees. High Fowler's is 60 to 90 degrees.

Why is Fowler's position used?

It helps the chest expand and makes breathing easier. High Fowler's also lowers the risk of aspiration when a feeding tube is placed.

What is the Trendelenburg position?

The whole bed tilts head-down. It is used in some abdominal surgery and to increase blood flow back to the heart, but only for limited times.

Which bed is needed for Fowler's position?

A Fowler bed raises the back-rest and knee-rest. A 5-function ICU bed also adjusts height and tilts for Trendelenburg.
