Newborn Hypothermia: Keeping Babies Warm and Using a Radiant Warmer

Newborns lose heat fast, and low body temperature raises the risk of death. This guide explains normal and low temperatures, the "warm chain" steps after birth, and how to use a radiant warmer, based on a Government of India standard treatment workflow.

A newborn's normal temperature is 36.5 to 37.5°C. Below 36.5°C is hypothermia: mild at 36.0 to 36.4°C, moderate at 32.0 to 35.9°C, and severe below 32°C. Why Newborns Get Cold

A newborn is wet at birth, so it loses heat quickly by evaporation, and also to cooler air and objects around it. Indian guidance states plainly that hypothermia in newborns increases deaths. Low-birth-weight and preterm babies are at the highest risk.

Measure with a standard digital thermometer, tip in the armpit, held parallel to the body, until it beeps.

The Warm Chain in the Delivery Room

  1. Keep the room free of draughts and above 25°C, with a room thermometer.
  2. Switch on the radiant warmer 20 to 30 minutes before delivery, in manual mode at 100% heat.
  3. Warm 2 to 3 sterile towels under the warmer for 20 minutes.
  4. Dry the baby at once and remove wet linen.
  5. Start skin-to-skin contact for stable babies for at least 1 hour, or until the first breastfeed.
  6. Check the temperature within the first hour.

On the ward, keep the baby covered, close windows and doors, switch off fans and air conditioners near the baby, start kangaroo mother care early for eligible babies, and delay the first bath until after discharge.

Using a Radiant Warmer

  • Manual mode: the heater runs at a set output. Used to pre-warm the bed and towels before birth.
  • Skin servo mode: a temperature probe is attached to the baby, and the warmer adjusts the heat to keep the skin at the set temperature. Used for babies with moderate hypothermia.
  • Monitoring: babies under a warmer or in an incubator need continuous temperature monitoring.

We suggest checking that the probe stays firmly attached, since a loose probe can read the warmer's heat instead of the baby's skin. Always follow the warmer maker's instructions for probe placement and alarms.

What to Do if a Baby Is Cold

  • Mild: warm the room to 25 to 28°C, give skin-to-skin contact and keep breastfeeding. Recheck in 1 hour.
  • Moderate: nurse under a radiant warmer in servo mode with a probe attached. Check blood sugar, give oxygen if SpO2 is below 91%, and recheck temperature every 15 minutes until normal.
  • Severe: treat as moderate, stop feeds, start IV fluids as advised by the doctor, and refer to a higher centre if the baby is in shock or has breathing failure.

Never place a newborn naked on a trolley or bed without a heat source. For transport, use skin-to-skin contact, a thermal mattress or a transport incubator.

Newborn Thermal Care: Key Facts

  • A newborn's normal axillary temperature is 36.5 to 37.5°C.
  • Hypothermia is mild at 36.0 to 36.4°C, moderate at 32.0 to 35.9°C and severe below 32°C.
  • Switch on the radiant warmer 20 to 30 minutes before delivery.
  • Keep the delivery room above 25°C and start skin-to-skin contact early.
  • Use skin servo mode with a probe for babies with moderate hypothermia.

Newborn Thermal Care: FAQs

What is the normal temperature of a newborn?

36.5 to 37.5 degrees C, measured in the armpit. Below 36.5 degrees C is hypothermia.

What are the grades of newborn hypothermia?

Mild is 36.0 to 36.4 degrees C, moderate is 32.0 to 35.9 degrees C, and severe is below 32 degrees C.

When should a radiant warmer be switched on before delivery?

Indian guidance says 20 to 30 minutes before delivery, in manual mode at 100% heat, with 2 to 3 towels warming under it.

What is skin servo mode on a baby warmer?

A probe on the baby's skin measures temperature, and the warmer adjusts its heat to keep the skin at the set level.

How do you warm a baby with mild hypothermia?

Warm the room to 25 to 28 degrees C, give skin-to-skin contact, keep breastfeeding and recheck the temperature in 1 hour.
