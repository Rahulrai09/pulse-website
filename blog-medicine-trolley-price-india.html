Medicine Trolley vs Crash Cart vs Instrument Trolley

  • Medicine trolley: for the daily medicine round on a ward. Drawers hold each patient's doses, and a lock keeps them safe.
  • Crash cart: for emergencies such as cardiac arrest. Drawers are set out for drugs, airway kit and IV fluids, with space for a defibrillator.
  • Instrument trolley: an open steel trolley that holds sterile instruments during a procedure or dressing. See our instrument trolley price guide.

Features That Matter

  • Lock: a central lock that closes all drawers with one key saves time and keeps drugs safe between rounds.
  • Drawers and dividers: movable dividers let you set out doses by patient or by bed number.
  • Frame: stainless steel lasts longer and does not rust after cleaning. Powder-coated mild steel costs less.
  • Top surface: an ABS or steel top gives a clean place to prepare doses and check charts.
  • Wheels: quiet wheels for night rounds, with brakes on at least two.
  • Size: check that it fits through ward doors and between beds.

NABH Rules for Storing Medicines

NABH's chapter on managing medicines (MOM) covers the whole cycle, from buying and storing drugs to giving them and watching for side effects. For trolleys and ward stock, a common NABH pharmacy checklist asks for:

  • Look-alike, sound-alike (LASA) drugs on a list, stored apart or colour coded, including in the fridge.
  • High-risk medicines on a list and kept in a protected place, so they are not given by mistake.
  • Narcotic (NDPS) drugs in a locked container, with a register of every dose issued.
  • Expiry checks: first in, first out, with near-expiry stock marked.
  • Storage as the maker advises: clean, dry and at the right temperature.

Before each dose, staff check the right patient, drug, dose, route and time. A trolley with labelled drawers and a lock makes these checks easier. For equipment records, see our NABH medical equipment requirements guide.

How Many Medicine Trolleys Do You Need?

We suggest at least one medicine trolley for each nursing station, one for each ICU, and a spare for large wards or busy shifts. Keep it separate from the crash cart, which must stay fully stocked for emergencies.

Medicine Trolley Price: FAQs

What is the price of a medicine trolley in India?

Online listings checked in November 2026 show basic mild steel ward trolleys at about Rs 7,000 to Rs 8,500, and medicine trolleys with lockable drawers at about Rs 10,000 to Rs 25,000.

What is the difference between a medicine trolley and a crash cart?

A medicine trolley is used for the daily medicine round and holds each patient's doses in locked drawers. A crash cart is kept ready for emergencies, with drugs, airway kit and space for a defibrillator.

What features should a medicine trolley have?

A central lock, drawers with movable dividers, a clean top surface, a rust-free frame and quiet wheels with brakes.

What does NABH say about storing medicines on wards?

NABH's medication chapter covers storage. A common checklist asks for LASA drugs to be stored apart or colour coded, high-risk drugs to be kept protected, narcotics locked with a register, and expiry dates checked.

How many medicine trolleys does a hospital need?

We suggest at least one for each nursing station and each ICU, plus a spare for large wards. Keep it separate from the crash cart.
