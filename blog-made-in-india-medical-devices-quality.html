Make in India Medical Devices: Is the Quality Actually There?

Short answer: some Indian-made medical devices meet demanding quality and regulatory standards, and some do not. "Made in India" alone is not proof of either. The country label tells you almost nothing on its own. Here is what actually determines quality, and how to check it yourself.

The Short Answer

India's medical device ecosystem in 2026 is genuinely mixed. Established manufacturers with strong quality systems, specialist innovators, capable MSMEs, private-label suppliers, and some low-transparency sellers all exist under the same "Made in India" label. Neither "imported is always better" nor "Indian products are automatically equal to global ones" holds up. What actually matters is the specific manufacturer, the specific product, and whether you have checked the documentation behind it.

What "Made in India" Actually Means

"Made in India" can describe very different manufacturing realities: a device engineered and fully made domestically, a device assembled in India from imported parts, or a device made under contract for a brand that never touches the factory floor. All three can legitimately carry a "Made in India" label. None of that tells you whether that specific product was tested, certified, or clinically evaluated to the standard your hospital needs.

How Medical Device Quality Is Actually Regulated in India

India regulates medical devices under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940 and the Medical Devices Rules, 2017. CDSCO classifies devices into four risk classes:

ClassRisk Level
Class ALow risk
Class BLow-moderate risk
Class CModerate-high risk
Class DHigh risk

Licensing responsibility for Class C and Class D manufacturing sits with the Central Licensing Authority. Lower-risk devices fall under other parts of the same regulatory framework. CDSCO guidance also references quality systems such as ISO 13485 for manufacturing-related evidence. But ISO 13485 certification is not a substitute for product-specific testing, regulatory approval, clinical evidence, or post-market surveillance. It is one input, not the whole answer.

India's National Medical Devices Policy, 2023 aims to reduce import dependence and build India into a global medical device manufacturing hub. The sector's growth reflects real investment: infrastructure like AMTZ (Andhra Pradesh MedTech Zone) now hosts more than 100 companies working on medical device research, development, and production.

Where Indian Manufacturing Is Genuinely Strong

With product-specific qualification still required in every case, these are categories where domestic manufacturing has built real depth:

  • Medical consumables and disposables
  • Infusion and blood-management products
  • Diagnostic reagents and laboratory equipment
  • Ophthalmic products
  • Some imaging and radiology equipment
  • Critical-care equipment
  • Contract manufacturing and engineering support
  • Cost-sensitive products designed specifically for Indian hospital conditions

This doesn't mean every domestic product in these categories carries identical export credentials or clinical evidence — it means these are areas where the ecosystem has matured furthest.

Where Buyers Should Ask Harder Questions

Scrutiny should increase, regardless of country of origin, when purchasing:

  • Ventilators and anaesthesia machines
  • Dialysis equipment
  • Implantable devices
  • Interventional cardiology devices
  • Neonatal equipment
  • High-end imaging systems
  • Life-support equipment
  • Software-connected or cybersecurity-sensitive devices

A Hospital Buyer's Quality Checklist

Before finalising any purchase — domestic or imported — ask:

  • What is the device's CDSCO classification?
  • Who is the legal manufacturer, and is their manufacturing licence current?
  • Does the ISO 13485 certificate cover this specific facility and product scope?
  • What testing standards apply, and is clinical-performance evidence available?
  • What is the documented failure rate and complaint history?
  • What is the mean time to repair, and are critical spare parts available in India?
  • Who performs installation and preventive maintenance?
  • Is there a documented recall and field-safety process?
  • Does the warranty include consumables, batteries, and accessories?
  • Can the manufacturer provide references from comparable hospitals?
Country of origin is a starting point for a conversation, not a substitute for it. The documentation is what actually tells you whether a device is reliable.

Make in India Medical Devices: Key Facts

  • Under India's Medical Devices Rules, 2017, devices fall into four risk classes: A (low), B (low-moderate), C (moderate-high) and D (high).
  • Over 4,000 medical device manufacturing sites were licensed in India between 2021 and 2025, the government told Parliament in December 2025.
  • India's National Medical Devices Policy, 2023 aims to grow the sector from about USD 11 billion to USD 50 billion by 2030.
  • The PLI scheme for medical devices has a Rs 3,420 crore outlay; 22 greenfield projects were commissioned by September 2025.
  • ISO 13485 certification is one input, not proof that a specific product is approved or clinically tested.

Make in India Medical Devices: FAQs

Is Indian-made medical equipment as reliable as imported equipment?

It depends entirely on the specific manufacturer and product. Some Indian manufacturers meet demanding international benchmarks; others don't yet have the track record. Country of origin alone isn't a reliable predictor either way.

Does ISO 13485 certification guarantee a device is safe?

No. It's evidence of a manufacturer's quality management system, not a guarantee about any specific product's safety or performance. Product-specific testing and clinical evidence matter separately.

What's the single most useful thing to check before buying?

The device's CDSCO classification and the manufacturing licence tied to that specific product — this tells you who is legally accountable and what regulatory tier the device falls under.
