Infant radiant warmers in India range from about Rs 23,500 to several lakh. This guide explains the price bands, the difference between manual and servo control, and what labour rooms and NICUs should check.

Manual vs Servo Control

  • Manual mode: the nurse sets the heater power. Simple, but the baby's temperature has to be watched closely.
  • Servo (skin) control: a probe on the baby's skin measures temperature and the warmer adjusts heat automatically to hold the set value. This is safer for small and sick newborns.

What Drives the Price

1. Temperature control

Air and skin servo control with digital displays of set and actual temperature cost more than manual warmers.

2. Alarms and self-test

High temperature, probe failure, system failure and power failure alarms protect the baby. Self-test at start-up catches faults early.

3. Phototherapy

Built-in LED phototherapy for jaundice saves buying a separate unit and saves space.

4. Bassinet and extras

Tilting bassinets, X-ray cassette trays, resuscitation modules, drawers and Apgar timers add cost and convenience.

5. Service

Heater elements and skin probes need replacement over time. Check availability and response time locally.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a baby warmer cost in India?

Online listings in October 2026 show basic infant radiant warmers at about Rs 23,500 to Rs 32,000, warmers with skin sensor and phototherapy at about Rs 59,000 to Rs 75,000, and advanced imported NICU warmers at several lakh.

What is a servo-controlled baby warmer?

A warmer that uses a skin temperature probe on the baby and automatically adjusts the heater to keep the baby at the set temperature, instead of the nurse setting heater power by hand.

Do I need a baby warmer with phototherapy?

A warmer with built-in LED phototherapy is useful where newborn jaundice is treated often and space is limited. Larger nurseries may prefer separate phototherapy units.

Where are baby warmers used?

In labour rooms and maternity OTs for newborn resuscitation and stabilisation, and in SNCUs and NICUs for sick or small babies who need warmth with easy access for care.

What should I check before buying a baby warmer?

Check manual and servo control, alarms (high temperature, probe and power failure), display, bassinet tilt and access, phototherapy option, X-ray tray, build quality and local service.
