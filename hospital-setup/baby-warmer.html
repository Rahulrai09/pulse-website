How a Radiant Baby Warmer Works

Newborns lose heat fast, especially premature and low birth weight babies. A radiant warmer has a heater above an open bed, so staff can reach the baby easily while the baby stays warm.

In servo (skin) mode, a small probe on the baby's skin reads the temperature and the warmer adjusts its heat to keep the baby at the set value. In manual mode, staff set the heater output themselves. The Premium N-400 adds LED phototherapy, the blue light used to treat newborn jaundice.

What to Check Before Buying a Baby Warmer

  • Servo control. Essential for NICUs and labour rooms that care for small or sick babies.
  • Alarms. High temperature, probe failure, system failure and power failure alarms are standard on the Premium models.
  • Phototherapy. Choose a model with LED phototherapy if you treat jaundice at the warmer.
  • Display. A dual display showing set and actual temperature makes checks quicker.
  • Bed and tray. Check the bassinet tilt, side panels and X-ray cassette tray if you need them.
  • Power. The Premium models draw about 650 W; plan power backup for NICUs.
