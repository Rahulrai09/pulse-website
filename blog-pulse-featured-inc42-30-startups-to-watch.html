Pulse Featured in Inc42's "30 Startups To Watch" (July 2026)

Inc42, one of India's most-read startup and business media outlets, named Pulse in its July 2026 edition of "30 Startups To Watch." Here is what the feature actually says, where it came from, and the funding and R&D context behind it.

What Is Inc42's "30 Startups To Watch"?

"30 Startups To Watch" is a recurring monthly feature published by Inc42, curating early-stage Indian startups its editorial team considers worth following. The July 2026 edition — titled "30 Startups To Watch: Startups That Caught Our Eye In July 2026" — was published on 3 August 2026, written by Akshit Pushkarna and Anjali Jain.

What Inc42 Said About Pulse

The article describes Pulse as a Bengaluru-based, full-stack, asset-light medical equipment company, founded in 2025 by Anshul Sharma and Nishant Goel. Per Inc42's reporting, Pulse designs, sources, and supplies medical devices and consumables through partnerships with Indian MSME (micro, small, and medium enterprise) manufacturers, while handling product design, quality systems, regulatory compliance, and after-sales service directly.

The piece places Pulse's work in the context of critical care, renal care, and cardiac care equipment, and cites the global medical devices market as valued at more than $600 billion. It also notes that Pulse works with close to 20 MSME partners across Delhi NCR, Gujarat, Kolkata, and Mumbai, and is setting up an R&D centre in Visakhapatnam.

The Funding Behind the Feature

Inc42 had separately reported on Pulse's funding in February 2026: a $4 million seed round led by 3one4 Capital, with participation from Incubate Fund Asia and Stride Ventures, along with angel investors. Reported use of funds includes an R&D hub, faster product development, regulatory approvals, and expanded distribution across India.

Why This Matters

Independent press coverage is a useful external check, not a substitute for a hospital's own due diligence. What a feature like this does confirm is that Pulse's manufacturer-first model, MSME network, and R&D plans have been reported on and described consistently by an outside publication — not just stated by Pulse itself. Buyers evaluating Pulse should still verify product-specific certification and compliance directly, the same as with any equipment vendor.

Inc42 described Pulse as building toward "a full-stack, asset-light medical equipment" model — sourcing and engineering through a network of Indian MSME manufacturing partners, rather than a single factory.
